## Open questions for review

Each has a recommendation. Decisions here change the draft below.

1. **Unnormalized inputs in the editor.** The taxonomy says nonphysical inputs are flagged, not silently accepted. *Recommendation:* let the learner enter any two real amplitudes, show a live normalization indicator (the sum of the squares), flag an invalid state in words and with an icon, and offer an explicit "Normalize" button. Show the probability bars always, with the total beside them, so the learner sees why an invalid state is invalid (the bars add to something other than 100%).
2. **Controls.** *Recommendation:* a slider for each amplitude (range −1 to 1, step 0.01) with a numeric readout, plus four preset buttons (|0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, |−⟩). Sliders are good for exploring but imprecise, so also allow typing a number.
3. **Introducing 1/√2.** |+⟩ needs 1/√2 ≈ 0.71. *Recommendation:* show it as "0.71, which is 1/√2" with one line saying 0.71 squared is about 0.5. Use the 3–4–5 pair (0.6 and 0.8, giving 36% and 64%) for every worked and checked example so the arithmetic stays clean.
4. **Column of two numbers.** The taxonomy lists it at Beginner as "listing only". It risks leading into vector arithmetic. *Recommendation:* show it once, as "another way to list the same two numbers," and say that nothing is calculated with it here. Matrices come in 02.04 at Intermediate.
5. **Naming |+⟩ and |−⟩ here.** 02.01 deliberately called the state "balanced superposition" and deferred the names to 02.02 and 02.03. *Recommendation:* introduce |+⟩ and |−⟩ on this page, as the balanced superposition with a plus sign and with a minus sign, and say that 02.01's balanced superposition is |+⟩.
6. **What distinguishes |+⟩ from |−⟩.** They give identical Z-basis counts. *Recommendation:* show that the probabilities are identical and the amplitude lists differ, call them different states, and say later pages (02.03, 02.04, 02.07) show how to tell them apart. Do not claim anything is distinguished on this page. This matches 02.01's Intermediate block "Same counts, different states".
7. **Build the Intermediate layer now?** The taxonomy puts complex amplitudes, modulus, bra notation, inner products, global versus relative phase, and a complex amplitude editor with a phase dial at Intermediate. *Recommendation:* specify it as one gated section and build it with the page, because the formal layer is the point of Module 02 and it is the first real use of a level-aware component. Cut the optional parts (inner products) if the page gets heavy.
8. **Shared amplitude model.** 02.03 (a sphere synchronized with the amplitudes), 02.04 (a gate and state explorer), and 02.07 will all need the same state math. *Recommendation:* create one small shared module, `QedQubit`, now, with unit tests in `tests/js` (this covers the taxonomy's quality gate for normalization and probability sums). It holds amplitude math only: normalization, probabilities, relative phase, and formatting. It does **not** include Bloch conversions or drawing; those belong with Trey's `QedBloch` once it is on `main`, so the two modules do not duplicate each other.
9. **Probability bars.** `QedShots.renderHTML` draws bars from counts. The editor needs bars from probabilities, with the same markup. *Recommendation:* expose the row rendering as a small helper that both modules use, rather than duplicating the markup. *Question:* put that helper in `QedShots`, or in a new shared bars module?
10. **Glossary.** Existing entries to reuse: Probability Amplitude, Phase, Relative Phase, Bra-Ket Notation, Computational Basis, Basis State, Quantum State, Hilbert Space. Missing and likely reusable: **Normalization** and **Global Phase**. *Recommendation:* add both. Consider *Inner Product* only if the optional Intermediate block is built.

## Goal

Teach the learner to write down the state of one qubit as two numbers, one for each basis state, and to use those numbers to check that the state is valid and to find the outcome probabilities.

Central progression:

`A STATE → TWO NUMBERS, ONE PER BASIS STATE → THEIR SQUARES ARE THE PROBABILITIES → THE SQUARES MUST ADD TO 1 → THE SIGN OF A NUMBER STILL MATTERS`

Key conceptual transition: **"A state can be written as two numbers. Their squares give the probabilities, and their signs carry something the probabilities do not show."**

Builds on 02.01's idea that a state is more than the outcomes it produces. Ends by asking how to picture a state, including its sign, instead of reading a list of numbers (02.03).

## Learning outcomes

**Beginner (the Core Learning Contract).** The learner should be able to:

1. Write a state as an amplitude for |0⟩ and an amplitude for |1⟩.
2. Read a ket such as |0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, or |−⟩ as a name for a particular pair of amplitudes.
3. Compute outcome probabilities from real amplitudes by squaring them.
4. Check whether a state is normalized (the squares add to 1) and say why it must be.
5. Explain that amplitudes are not probabilities: the probability is the amplitude squared.
6. Explain that a negative amplitude is a real difference in the state, even when the probabilities do not change.
7. Identify |+⟩ and |−⟩ as different states that give the same Z-basis counts.
8. State that real amplitudes are a simplification, and that some states need more than real numbers.
9. Ask how a state, including its sign, can be pictured instead of read from a list of numbers.

**Intermediate (gated).** Work with complex amplitudes and their modulus, write |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ with |α|² + |β|² = 1, and distinguish global phase from relative phase.

**Advanced (gated).** Name Hilbert-space language, dual vectors, Hermitian conjugation, and global-phase equivalence.

## Experience-level scope

**Beginner (V1) teaches:** a state as two real amplitudes; kets as names; probability as amplitude squared; normalization as "the squares add to 1"; the sign of an amplitude; |+⟩ versus |−⟩; one sentence that real amplitudes are a simplification; the column of two numbers as another way to list the amplitudes (listing only).

**Intermediate (gated now):** complex numbers and modulus (a short scaffold); complex amplitudes; |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ and |α|² + |β|² = 1; global versus relative phase; a complex amplitude editor with a phase dial. Optional: bra notation and inner products, orthonormality of |0⟩ and |1⟩.

**Advanced (named only):** Hilbert-space language, dual vectors, Hermitian conjugation, global-phase equivalence classes.

**Not on this page (any level):** gates, matrices, the Bloch sphere, tensor products, the Born rule as a general formula beyond squaring amplitudes, and measurement formalism. Those belong to 02.03, 02.04, 02.05, and 02.07.

## Page structure (draft)

1. **From a Prepared State to Numbers.** Reconnect to 02.01: a preparation produces a state, and a state comes with numbers that decide the probabilities. Ask: "How can we write a state down so we can calculate what it will do?" Introduce the idea: one number for |0⟩ and one for |1⟩.

2. **Two Numbers for Two Basis States.** Show the state as a|0⟩ + b|1⟩ with real a and b. A ket is a name for a state. Show |0⟩ = (1, 0), |1⟩ = (0, 1), |+⟩ = (0.71, 0.71), |−⟩ = (0.71, −0.71), and show the column of two numbers once as another way to list the same pair, with nothing calculated from it. Say that real amplitudes are a simplification.

3. **Main Interactive: "Amplitude Editor."** The learner edits the two amplitudes and watches the written form, the normalization indicator, and the probability bars. See below.

4. **Probabilities from Amplitudes.** Probability is the amplitude squared. Worked example with 0.6 and 0.8: 36% and 64%. Normalization: the probabilities must add to 100%, so the squares must add to 1. A valid state is one whose probabilities add up. Normalization is a requirement on valid states, not a free choice of numbers.

5. **The Sign Matters.** Compare |+⟩ and |−⟩ in the editor. The bars are identical and the amplitude lists differ, so they are different states with the same Z-basis counts. Connect to 02.01's "same counts, different states". Do not show how to tell them apart; later pages do.

6. **Beyond the Core.** Gated block (heading inside the Intermediate block, as on 02.01).
   - `[Intermediate]` Complex numbers and modulus, a short scaffold. Complex amplitudes. |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩, |α|² + |β|² = 1. Global versus relative phase. A complex amplitude editor with a phase dial. Optional: bra notation, inner products, orthonormality.
   - `[Advanced]` A very short block naming Hilbert-space language, dual vectors, Hermitian conjugation, and global-phase equivalence.

7. **From Writing Down to Picturing.** Bridge. A list of numbers is hard to read at a glance, and a sign is easy to miss. Ask: "How can we picture a state, including its sign, instead of reading a list of numbers?" Next: **Bloch Sphere →**

## Main Interactive: "Amplitude Editor"

- **Controls:** a slider (with a numeric readout and a typed value) for each amplitude, from −1 to 1; presets for |0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, and |−⟩; "Normalize"; reset.
- **Live readouts**, kept in separate places:
  - the written form, for example 0.60|0⟩ + 0.80|1⟩;
  - the normalization indicator: the sum of the squares, with "valid state" or "not a valid state (the probabilities add to X%)" in words and an icon;
  - the probability bars for outcome 0 and outcome 1, each with its percentage.
- **Invalid states:** flagged in words, not silently accepted or silently corrected. "Normalize" rescales both amplitudes so the squares add to 1 and keeps their signs, and says so.
- **Zero state:** if both amplitudes are zero, say that nothing can be normalized, and do not divide by zero.
- **Presets** set the amplitudes and show the name (|0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, |−⟩) next to the written form.
- **Intermediate behavior (level-aware, gated):** at Intermediate and above the editor adds, for each amplitude, a phase control, shows the modulus, and shows the relative phase. A "global phase" control shifts both phases together and shows that the bars and the relative phase do not change. The Beginner behavior never depends on the level.
- **Accessibility:** keyboard-operable controls; every bar paired with a text percentage; a text summary of the current state and its validity in an aria-live region; a reset that returns the starting state.
- **Scope fence:** no gates, no Bloch sphere, no shots (02.07 owns sampling here).

### Checks (taxonomy section 7)

- *Prediction task:* given the amplitudes 0.6 and 0.8, predict the probabilities (36% and 64%) before the bars appear, then compare.
- *Conceptual question:* which of several amplitude pairs is a valid state? Feedback explains each choice (for example 0.6 and 0.6 add to 0.72, not 1).
- *Transfer task:* make outcome 1 happen 36% of the time. Several amplitude pairs work (for example 0.8 and 0.6, or 0.8 and −0.6). The learner finds one in the editor, and the feedback notes that both signs give the same probabilities.

## Important pedagogical constraints (draft)

Do not imply:
- `AMPLITUDE = PROBABILITY`. The probability is the amplitude squared, and an amplitude can be negative.
- `ANY TWO NUMBERS ARE A STATE`. A valid state is normalized; normalization comes from probabilities adding to 1, not from an arbitrary choice.
- `A NEGATIVE AMPLITUDE MEANS NEGATIVE PROBABILITY`, or that the sign does nothing because the probabilities do not change.
- `REAL AMPLITUDES ARE ALL THERE IS`. Say plainly that real amplitudes are a simplification.
- That |+⟩ and |−⟩ can be told apart by Z-basis counts, or that this page shows how to tell them apart.
- That the column of two numbers is something to calculate with here.

Do not teach here: gates, matrices, matrix–vector multiplication, the Bloch sphere, tensor products, or measurement formalism.

## End state (draft)

The learner leaves with: `A STATE → TWO NUMBERS → THEIR SQUARES ARE THE PROBABILITIES → THEY MUST ADD TO 1 → THE SIGN CARRIES SOMETHING THE PROBABILITIES DO NOT SHOW`.

Takeaway: *A qubit's state can be written as two amplitudes. Their squares give the probabilities and must add to 1, and the signs carry information the probabilities do not show.*

Ends with: "How can we picture a state, including its sign, instead of reading a list of numbers?" and **Next: Bloch Sphere →**
