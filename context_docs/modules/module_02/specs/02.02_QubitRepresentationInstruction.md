This section continues Module 02 — Quantum Computing Fundamentals. 02.01 separated a state from an outcome and mentioned amplitudes and phase only in words. This page gives them numbers.
________________________________________
Goal
Teach the learner how to write the state of one qubit as two numbers, building on 02.01's idea that a state is more than the outcomes it produces.
The central progression is:
A STATE → TWO NUMBERS, ONE PER BASIS STATE → THEIR SQUARES ARE THE PROBABILITIES → THE SQUARES MUST ADD TO 1 → THE SIGN OF A NUMBER STILL MATTERS
The learner should understand that the numbers, called amplitudes, decide the probabilities, and that they carry more than the probabilities show.
The key conceptual transition is:
“A state can be written as two numbers. Their squares give the probabilities, and their signs carry something the probabilities do not show.”
This section should prepare the learner for Bloch Sphere (02.03).
________________________________________
Learning Outcomes
By the end of the section, the learner should be able to:
	Write a state as an amplitude for |0⟩ and an amplitude for |1⟩.
	Read a ket such as |0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, or |−⟩ as a name for a particular pair of amplitudes.
	Compute outcome probabilities from real amplitudes by squaring them.
	Check whether a state is normalized, meaning the squares add to 1, and say why it must be.
	Explain that an amplitude is not a probability: the probability is the amplitude squared.
	Explain that a negative amplitude is a real difference in the state, even when the probabilities do not change.
	Identify |+⟩ and |−⟩ as different states that give the same Z-basis counts.
	State that real amplitudes are a simplification, and that some states need more than real numbers.
	Ask how a state, including its sign, can be pictured instead of read from a list of numbers.

Page Structure
1. From a Prepared State to Numbers
Begin directly from the Quantum States page.
Revisit the familiar progression:
A PREPARATION PRODUCES A STATE
↓
A STATE COMES WITH NUMBERS THAT DECIDE THE PROBABILITIES
Then ask:
“How can we write a state down so we can calculate what it will do?”
Introduce the idea of one number for |0⟩ and one for |1⟩.
The learner should see these numbers as a precise version of what 02.01 described in words rather than as a new kind of thing.

2. Two Numbers for Two Basis States
Introduce the written form a|0⟩ + b|1⟩ with real a and b.
Clearly distinguish:
	Ket — a name for a state, such as |0⟩ or |+⟩.
	Amplitude — one of the two numbers that describe the state.
	Probability — the chance of an outcome, found from an amplitude.
Show |0⟩ = (1, 0), |1⟩ = (0, 1), |+⟩ = (0.71, 0.71), and |−⟩ = (0.71, −0.71).
Say that 02.01's balanced superposition is |+⟩.
Show the column of two numbers once, as another way to list the same pair. Say that nothing is calculated with it here.
Say plainly that real amplitudes are a simplification.
Do not imply that all states can be written with real numbers.
Include a short explanation such as:
“Why does |+⟩ show 0.71?”
“Its amplitudes are 1/√2, which is about 0.71. Squared, 0.71 gives about 0.5, so each outcome is about 50% likely.”

3. Main Interactive — “Amplitude Editor”
Make this the primary V1 interaction.
Allow the learner to edit the two amplitudes and watch the written form, the validity check, and the probability bars.
At minimum support:
	A slider for each amplitude, from −1 to 1 in steps of 0.01, with a numeric readout and a typed value.
	Preset buttons for |0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, and |−⟩.
	A “Normalize” button.
	Resetting the view to its starting state.
When the learner changes an amplitude:
	Show the written form, for example 0.60|0⟩ + 0.80|1⟩.
	Show the validity check: the sum of the squares, with “valid state” or “not a valid state, the probabilities add to X%” in words and with an icon.
	Show the probability bars for outcome 0 and outcome 1, each with its percentage.
	Show the written form, the validity check, and the bars in three separate places.
When the state is not valid:
	Flag it in words. Do not accept it silently and do not correct it silently.
	Offer “Normalize,” which rescales both amplitudes so the squares add to 1, keeps their signs, and says so.
When both amplitudes are zero:
	Say that nothing can be normalized. Do not divide by zero.
When the learner selects a preset:
	Set the amplitudes and show the name next to the written form.
The learner should visually experience:
CHANGE AN AMPLITUDE
↓
SEE THE WRITTEN FORM, THE TOTAL, AND THE BARS UPDATE
↓
SEE WHETHER THE STATE IS VALID
Reinforce the distinction established in 02.01:
A state is what was prepared; its amplitudes are the numbers that describe it.
Make the interaction resettable and allow learners to experiment with signs, sizes, and the presets.
At Intermediate and above, as a level-aware behavior gated through QedLevel:
	Add a phase control for each amplitude.
	Show the modulus of each amplitude and the relative phase.
	Add a global phase control that shifts both phases together, and show that the bars and the relative phase do not change.
	Show ⟨ψ|ψ⟩, ⟨0|ψ⟩, and ⟨1|ψ⟩ as numbers beside the written form, using the same bracketed form as complex amplitudes.
The Beginner behavior must never depend on the level.
Make the interaction keyboard operable. Pair every bar with a text percentage. Give a text summary of the current state and its validity in an aria-live region.
Do not add gates, the Bloch sphere, or shots here.

4. Probabilities from Amplitudes
Reconnect to the learner's knowledge from 02.01 that a state decides the pattern of outcomes.
Introduce probability as the amplitude squared.
Work one example with the 3–4–5 pair: amplitudes 0.6 and 0.8 give 36% and 64%.
The learner should understand:
AMPLITUDES
↓
SQUARE EACH ONE
↓
THE PROBABILITIES, WHICH MUST ADD TO 100%
Explain that normalization is a requirement on a valid state, not a free choice of numbers: the probabilities of all outcomes must add to 100%, so the squares must add to 1.
Include three checks, each with immediate explanatory feedback that explains every wrong choice:
	A prediction task: given amplitudes 0.6 and 0.8, predict the probabilities before the bars appear, then compare.
	A conceptual question: which of several amplitude pairs is a valid state? For example, 0.6 and 0.6 add to 0.72, not 1.
	A transfer task: make outcome 1 happen 36% of the time. Several pairs work, for example 0.8 and 0.6, or 0.8 and −0.6. The feedback notes that both signs give the same probabilities.
Do not require vectors, matrices, or any other calculation at Beginner level.

5. The Sign Matters
Compare |+⟩ and |−⟩ in the editor.
Show that the bars are identical and the amplitude lists differ.
Introduce the idea:
	Sign — a negative amplitude is a real difference in the state, even when the probabilities do not change.
The learner should discover that two different states can give identical Z-basis counts.
Connect this directly to 02.01:
These are the same-counts, different-states idea from the previous page, now with numbers.
Ask:
“If the probabilities are the same, in what way are |+⟩ and |−⟩ different?”
Answer only that they are different states with different amplitudes, and that later pages show how to tell them apart.
Do not claim that anything distinguishes them on this page.
Do not teach gates, interference, or the Bloch sphere yet.

6. Beyond the Core
Build the heading inside the Intermediate block, as on 02.01, so a Beginner sees neither an empty heading nor a table-of-contents entry for it.
[Intermediate] The Formal Layer
Add a gated block that covers:
	A short scaffold on complex numbers and modulus.
	Complex amplitudes, and |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ with |α|² + |β|² = 1.
	Global phase versus relative phase: a shared phase factor changes nothing observable, and the relative phase does.
	The editor's phase, modulus, relative-phase, and global-phase controls.
[Intermediate] Bra Notation and Inner Products
Add a second gated block after the formal layer.
Introduce, in this order:
	Bra — the bra ⟨ψ| is the partner of the ket |ψ⟩: the same amplitudes with each one replaced by its complex conjugate. For real amplitudes the numbers do not change.
	Inner product — ⟨φ|ψ⟩ is one number: multiply the conjugate of each amplitude of φ by the matching amplitude of ψ, then add the two products.
Show three results, with the editor's readouts beside them:
	⟨ψ|ψ⟩ = |α|² + |β|², so normalization says ⟨ψ|ψ⟩ = 1.
	⟨0|ψ⟩ = α and ⟨1|ψ⟩ = β: the inner product with a basis state reads off that basis state's amplitude.
	⟨0|0⟩ = ⟨1|1⟩ = 1 and ⟨0|1⟩ = 0: the basis states are orthonormal, meaning normalized and with inner product zero.
Optionally note that |+⟩ and |−⟩ are also orthonormal.
Explain the idea visually, through the editor's readouts, rather than relying on the equations.
Do not state the probability formula P = |⟨k|ψ⟩|². The general Born rule belongs to 02.07.
Do not describe the inner product as a row-times-column or matrix product. Matrices belong to 02.04.
Do not teach Hilbert-space or dual-space formalism here. The Advanced block only names it.
[Advanced] Naming the Formalism
Add a very short gated block naming Hilbert-space language, dual vectors, Hermitian conjugation, and global-phase equivalence, with no formalism.
Build both as gated blocks as described in @llm/CONVENTIONS.md section 7.

7. From Writing Down to Picturing
End by comparing what the learner can do now with what is still hard.
So far:
WRITE A STATE AS TWO NUMBERS
↓
CALCULATE THE PROBABILITIES
↓
CHECK THAT THE STATE IS VALID
versus:
SEE A STATE AT A GLANCE
↓
SEE ITS SIGN WITHOUT READING NUMBERS
↓
SEE HOW STATES RELATE TO EACH OTHER
Present a final example with |+⟩ and |−⟩ side by side as lists of numbers.
Ask:
“How can we picture a state, including its sign, instead of reading a list of numbers?”
Then introduce the next problem:
A list of numbers hides the sign and is hard to compare. We need a picture.
Do not explain the Bloch sphere here.
End with:
“How can we picture a state, including its sign, instead of reading a list of numbers?”

Important Pedagogical Constraints
Do not imply:
AMPLITUDE = PROBABILITY
Instead teach:
The probability is the amplitude squared, and an amplitude can be negative.
Do not imply:
ANY TWO NUMBERS = A STATE
Instead teach:
A valid state is normalized. Normalization comes from the probabilities adding to 100%, not from an arbitrary choice of numbers.
Do not imply:
A NEGATIVE AMPLITUDE = A NEGATIVE PROBABILITY
Instead teach:
The sign does not change the probability, but it is a real difference in the state.
Do not imply that real amplitudes are all there is. Say plainly that they are a simplification.
Do not imply that the sign does nothing because the probabilities do not change.
Do not imply that |+⟩ and |−⟩ can be told apart by Z-basis counts, or that this page shows how to tell them apart.
Do not imply that the column of two numbers is something to calculate with here.
Do not teach gates, matrices, matrix–vector multiplication, the Bloch sphere, tensor products, or measurement formalism here.
Keep Beginner V1 focused on the mental model required to understand why a state needs a picture.
