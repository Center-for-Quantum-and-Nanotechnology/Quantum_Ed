## Open questions for review

Each has a recommendation. Say which you accept or change; the rest of the draft already assumes the recommendations.

1. **Where does two-qubit code live?** The taxonomy reserves "controlled gates and two-qubit states" as extension points of `QedGates` and `QedQubit`, and 02.06 (the circuit trace) and 02.07 (joint measurement) will need the same objects. *Recommendation:* a **new shared module `QedTwoQubit` (`twoqubit.js`)** for the state of exactly two qubits, with the same narrow scope as `QedQubit` ("one qubit only"): four amplitudes, `product(a, b)`, applying a single-qubit gate to q0 or q1, applying a controlled gate, joint probabilities, each qubit's own probabilities (the marginals), a written form, and the separability test. `QedGates` gains the two-qubit *operations*: `kron`, `controlled(gate)`, and `CNOT`. Both are pure, with tests in `tests/js/twoqubit.test.js` and `gates.test.js`.

2. **How much can the explorer do?** The taxonomy asks for: choose single-qubit inputs, optionally apply H to q0 and then CNOT, see the joint probability table, and compare a product state with the Bell state. *Recommendation:* exactly that, as **two toggles in a fixed order**: "Apply H to q0", then "Apply CNOT (q0 control, q1 target)". No free chain builder; that is 02.06's job, and a fixed pipeline keeps the page about joint outcomes rather than about building circuits.

3. **Which single-qubit inputs at Beginner?** *Recommendation:* **|0⟩, |1⟩, and |+⟩ for each qubit at Beginner.** With |+⟩ on the control and |−⟩ in the target, CNOT changes the state (the minus sign moves over to the control, giving |−⟩ and |−⟩) but leaves every joint probability at 25%, so a probability table cannot show that anything happened, and a Beginner would read it as "CNOT did nothing". |−⟩ and the two complex presets (|+i⟩, |−i⟩) arrive at Intermediate, where the four-amplitude readout shows what happened. Record this in `llm/FLAGS.md`.

4. **What does the Beginner display show?** *Recommendation:* the **four joint probability bars** in the order 00, 01, 10, 11 (through `QedShots.renderBars`), each with a percentage; the **four amplitudes as a plain labelled list** (real numbers, listing only, as on 02.02); and each qubit's **own probabilities** (the marginals). The **2×2 table with row and column totals**, and the table of what independent qubits would give, appear in a "Product test" panel that the learner switches on. No Bloch sphere on this page (see question 8).

5. **How is the product test stated?** The taxonomy's test is "if the qubits were independent, P(00) would be P(0) × P(0) = 1/4, so the Bell outcomes cannot come from two independent qubits". *Recommendation:* state it **one way only**: independent qubits always satisfy the product rule, so a joint table that breaks it cannot belong to independent qubits. Do **not** say the converse. A table that passes the product rule does not by itself prove independence (for example, amplitudes ½, ½, ½, −½ give 25% for every outcome but are entangled). The page never claims otherwise; the exact test with amplitudes, αδ = βγ, is Intermediate.

6. **Qubit order and the software note.** The FLAGS heads-up says to state the convention explicitly here and to note that tools may differ. *Recommendation:* state the convention in one box: **the first qubit, q0, is written first (leftmost), is the CNOT control, and is the top wire**; the four outcomes are 00, 01, 10, 11 with the left digit for q0. Add one sentence that **some software libraries write bit strings with qubit 0 on the right**, so check the documentation of any tool before reading a bit string. Do not name or characterize a particular vendor without checking its current documentation. Tell the Module 03 author, as FLAGS says.

7. **Shots on this page?** *Recommendation:* **no.** The joint tables are the theoretical probabilities. Sampled counts, histograms, and the "no communication between measurements" account of Bell correlations are 02.07's. `QedShots` is used only for the bars and the outcome labels.

8. **Bloch sphere on this page?** *Recommendation:* **no spheres.** After CNOT, an entangled pair has a state, but neither qubit alone has a point on a sphere. Drawing a sphere per qubit would suggest otherwise. Instead say plainly, as the payoff of the limit named on 02.03, that neither qubit alone has a state of its own to draw. (At Intermediate this is the "several qubits do not fit on one sphere" limit; the mixed-state account is Advanced and named only.)

9. **Teaching CNOT with a control in superposition.** *Recommendation:* first the **truth table on basis states** (control 0: target unchanged; control 1: target flipped), then one sentence that **CNOT acts on each part of a superposition separately**: the part with the control at 0 is untouched, and the part with the control at 1 has its target flipped. The explorer lets the learner see this with |+⟩ on the control. No matrices at Beginner.

10. **Overlap with Module 01.** Module 01's Entanglement section already shows a Bell pair ("each random alone, always agree") through the CHSH game. *Recommendation:* open by **naming that pair and the CHSH game**, and say that this page computes the same pair from joint outcomes. Do not repeat the CHSH game or its numbers.

11. **How the Bell state is named.** *Recommendation:* at Beginner call it "**the Bell state**" and describe it by its outcome table (00 and 11 each 50%, 01 and 10 never). The written form (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 is Intermediate. The page shows the state it produces from |00⟩, the recurring example, and also the anti-correlated variant that the transfer task reaches.

12. **What Intermediate adds.** *Recommendation:* two gated blocks, plus level-aware behavior in the explorer. Block one is *Tensor products and four-component vectors*: the product of two states as a four-component vector in the order 00, 01, 10, 11; |+⟩⊗|0⟩ = (|00⟩ + |10⟩)/√2; H on q0 as H⊗I, with the note that the first factor is q0. Block two is *CNOT as a matrix, the Bell state, and separability*: the 4×4 CNOT matrix in that basis order; its action on a general state (α|00⟩ + β|01⟩ + γ|10⟩ + δ|11⟩ goes to α|00⟩ + β|01⟩ + δ|10⟩ + γ|11⟩); deriving the Bell state step by step; the separability test αδ = βγ with the three worked cases (the Bell state, |+⟩⊗|+⟩, and the entangled state with 25% everywhere). In the explorer, Intermediate adds |−⟩, |+i⟩, |−i⟩ as inputs, the four-component vector, the matrix panel, and the number αδ − βγ. Beginner never depends on any of this. Advanced names the Schmidt decomposition, entanglement measures, and the general controlled-U gate.

## Goal

Teach the learner what changes when two qubits are considered together: the outcomes become pairs, independent qubits multiply, and CNOT can produce a pair whose outcomes cannot come from two independent qubits.

Central progression:

`TWO QUBITS → FOUR JOINT OUTCOMES → INDEPENDENT QUBITS MULTIPLY → CNOT FLIPS ONE QUBIT IF THE OTHER IS 1 → H THEN CNOT GIVES THE BELL STATE → THE PRODUCT TEST FAILS`

Key conceptual transition: **"Two qubits have four joint outcomes. If they were independent, the joint probabilities would multiply. The Bell state's do not, so entanglement is not two independent superpositions."**

Builds on 02.04's closing question and its two qubits side by side. Ends by asking how these steps can be written down as a diagram (02.06).

## Learning outcomes

**Beginner (the Core Learning Contract).** The learner should be able to:

1. List the four joint outcomes of two qubits, 00, 01, 10, and 11, and state the order convention: q0 first, and the control.
2. Read a joint probability table, and read each qubit's own probabilities off it.
3. Explain that for independent qubits the joint probability is the product of the separate probabilities, and compute the joint table of two independent qubits.
4. Describe CNOT as a conditional flip with a control and a target, and trace it on basis states with a truth table.
5. Explain that CNOT acts on each part of a superposition separately.
6. Produce the Bell state's outcome table from |00⟩ with H on q0 and then CNOT: 00 and 11 each 50%.
7. Apply the product test and explain why the Bell outcomes cannot come from two independent qubits.
8. Explain that entanglement is not two independent superpositions, using |+⟩ and |+⟩ against the Bell state: each qubit alone is 50/50 in both.
9. State that neither qubit of an entangled pair has a state of its own to draw on the sphere, and that some software libraries order bit strings differently.
10. Ask how these steps can be written down as a diagram.

**Intermediate (gated).** Write a product state as a four-component vector; write H on q0 as H⊗I; write the CNOT matrix and apply it to a general state; derive the Bell state; test separability with αδ = βγ.

**Advanced (gated).** Name the Schmidt decomposition, entanglement measures, and controlled-U.

## Experience-level scope

**Beginner (V1) teaches:** joint outcomes and the order convention; joint probability tables and each qubit's own probabilities; independent qubits and the product rule; CNOT as a conditional flip with a truth table; the Bell state from H then CNOT as an outcome table; the product test and why Bell fails it; |+⟩ and |+⟩ against the Bell state; the limit of the one-qubit picture; the software-order note.

**Intermediate (gated now):** tensor products and four-component vectors; |+⟩⊗|0⟩ = (|00⟩ + |10⟩)/√2; H⊗I; the CNOT matrix and its action on a general state; deriving the Bell state; the separability test; the explorer's extra inputs, amplitude vector, matrix panel, and separability number.

**Advanced (named only):** Schmidt decomposition and entanglement measures; the controlled-U generalization.

**Not on this page (any level):** the CHSH game and Bell-inequality numbers (Module 01), shots, histograms, the Born rule, and the "no communication" account (02.07), the circuit builder and tracing (02.06), three or more qubits, SWAP, CZ, or other named two-qubit gates, stabilizers, syndromes, and encoding (Module 07), noise and decoherence (Module 04).

## Page structure (draft)

1. **From One Qubit to Two.** Reconnect to 02.04's two qubits side by side. Ask: "What changes when there is more than one qubit?" Name Module 01's Bell pair (each random alone, always agreeing) and the CHSH game, and say that this page computes the pair from joint outcomes.

2. **Joint Outcomes.** Two qubits give four outcomes: 00, 01, 10, 11. State the convention in one box: q0 first (leftmost), q0 is the CNOT control, and q0 is the top wire. Note that some software libraries order bit strings the other way. Show a joint probability table, with each qubit's own probabilities read off it.

3. **Independent Qubits.** If the qubits are independent, the joint probability is the product of the separate probabilities. Worked example: q0 in |+⟩ and q1 in |0⟩ give 50% for 00 and 50% for 10. Another: both in |+⟩ give 25% for every outcome. Show the product rule as a small table, and say that it holds for independent qubits.

4. **CNOT: A Conditional Flip.** Introduce control and target and a truth table on basis states (00 to 00, 01 to 01, 10 to 11, 11 to 10). Say that CNOT acts on each part of a superposition separately. Show the CNOT symbol through `QedCircuit` (control dot and target ⊕). Do not show a matrix.

5. **Main Interactive: "Two-Qubit Explorer."** Choose the two inputs, optionally apply H to q0, then optionally apply CNOT, and watch the joint probability table. A product-test panel compares the result with what independent qubits would give. See below.

6. **The Bell State.** Start from |00⟩, apply H to q0, then CNOT. The outcome table has 00 and 11 at 50% and 01 and 10 at 0%. Apply the product test: each qubit alone is 50/50, so independent qubits would give 25% for every outcome, but the table has 50%, 0%, 0%, 50%. Contrast with |+⟩ and |+⟩: each qubit alone is also 50/50, and the joint table does match the product. Entanglement is not two independent superpositions. Say that neither qubit alone has a state of its own to draw on the sphere. Checks (see below).

7. **Beyond the Core.** Gated blocks (heading inside the Intermediate block, as on 02.01 to 02.04).
   - `[Intermediate]` *Tensor products and four-component vectors.*
   - `[Intermediate]` *CNOT as a matrix, the Bell state, and separability.*
   - `[Advanced]` A very short block naming the Schmidt decomposition, entanglement measures, and controlled-U.

8. **From Two Qubits to Circuits.** Bridge. We can now describe two qubits, link them with CNOT, and make a Bell state, but the steps are described in words and tables. Ask: "How can we write these steps down as a diagram?" Next: **Quantum Circuits →**

## Main Interactive: "Two-Qubit Explorer"

- **Controls:**
  - the input for q0 and the input for q1: |0⟩, |1⟩, or |+⟩ at Beginner (question 3);
  - a toggle "Apply H to q0", and a toggle "Apply CNOT (q0 control, q1 target)", applied in that order;
  - a switch for the product test panel;
  - reset, which returns to |0⟩ and |0⟩ with both toggles off.
- **Display, kept in separate places:**
  - the **steps** as a two-wire circuit through `QedCircuit` (H on q0, then CNOT), with the inputs named beside the wires;
  - the **four joint probability bars**, in the order 00, 01, 10, 11, with percentages;
  - the **four amplitudes** as a plain labelled list (real numbers, listing only);
  - each qubit's **own probabilities**;
  - the **product test panel**: the actual 2×2 table with row and column totals beside the table that independent qubits with the same own probabilities would give, and a one-sentence verdict.
- **Verdict wording:** "These match what independent qubits would give" or "These do not match what independent qubits would give, so the qubits are not independent". The verdict never claims independence from a match alone (question 5).
- **Reading it in words:** a one-line caption, such as "H made q0 an even mix. CNOT then copied the choice onto q1, so 00 and 11 are the only outcomes."
- **Intermediate behavior (level-aware):** |−⟩, |+i⟩, and |−i⟩ become inputs; a four-component vector; a matrix panel (the 4×4 matrix of the step, the input vector, the output vector); the number αδ − βγ with a note on what zero means. The Beginner behavior never depends on the level.
- **Accessibility:** the buttons and toggles are keyboard-operable and work without any drawing. The circuit has a text description, the bars carry text percentages, and an aria-live region summarizes each change, including the verdict.
- **Scope fence:** no shots or sampling, no Bloch spheres, no free chain of gates, no third qubit, no other two-qubit gate.

### Checks (taxonomy section 7)

- *Prediction task:* "q0 is in |+⟩ and q1 is in |0⟩. You apply CNOT. Predict the joint probabilities" (50% for 00 and 50% for 11), then compare with the bars. Feedback names the Bell state and notes that the qubits are now linked.
- *Conceptual question:* which of several joint tables could come from two independent qubits? (25% each, and 50% for 00 with 50% for 10, could. 50% for 00 with 50% for 11, and 50% for 01 with 50% for 10, could not.) Feedback explains each choice with the product rule.
- *Transfer task:* make 01 and 10 each happen 50% of the time, with 00 and 11 never. A solution: start with q0 in |0⟩ and q1 in |1⟩, apply H to q0, then CNOT. The feedback notes that the outcomes are always opposite and that the product test fails here too.

## Important pedagogical constraints (draft)

Do not imply:
- `ENTANGLEMENT = TWO INDEPENDENT SUPERPOSITIONS`. |+⟩ and |+⟩ are two independent superpositions and the Bell state is not.
- `THE BELL STATE IS TWO QUBITS THAT HAPPEN TO AGREE`, or that the qubits carry a hidden agreed answer. How the correlations are measured, and why they are not a hidden answer, is 02.07 and Module 01.
- `PASSING THE PRODUCT TEST PROVES INDEPENDENCE`. Only failing it proves something at Beginner (question 5).
- That CNOT copies a qubit, or that it "reads" the control. It flips the target on the part of the state where the control is 1, and it can be undone.
- That each qubit of an entangled pair has its own state. Neither has one to draw on the sphere.
- That the qubit order is universal. It is the convention on this page, and some software libraries write bit strings the other way.
- That outcome order 01 means the same qubit in every tool.
- That this page shows how to measure the pair. The tables are the theoretical probabilities, and sampled counts are on the measurement page.

Do not teach here: the CHSH game, shots and histograms, the Born rule as a formula, the circuit builder, three or more qubits, other named two-qubit gates, stabilizers, syndromes, noise, or decoherence.

## End state (draft)

The learner leaves with: `TWO QUBITS GIVE FOUR JOINT OUTCOMES → INDEPENDENT QUBITS MULTIPLY → CNOT FLIPS THE TARGET IF THE CONTROL IS 1 → H THEN CNOT GIVES THE BELL STATE → THE PRODUCT TEST FAILS, SO IT IS NOT TWO INDEPENDENT QUBITS`.

Takeaway: *Two qubits have four joint outcomes. Independent qubits multiply their probabilities, and CNOT after H gives a pair whose outcomes do not, so entanglement is not two independent superpositions.*
