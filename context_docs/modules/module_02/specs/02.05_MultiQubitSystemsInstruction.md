This section continues Module 02 — Quantum Computing Fundamentals. 02.04 gave one qubit's state a way to move and ended with two qubits side by side. This page asks what changes when the qubits are considered together.
________________________________________
Goal
Teach the learner what changes when two qubits are considered together, building on 02.04's one-qubit gates.
The central progression is:
TWO QUBITS → FOUR JOINT OUTCOMES → INDEPENDENT QUBITS MULTIPLY → CNOT FLIPS ONE QUBIT IF THE OTHER IS 1 → H THEN CNOT GIVES THE BELL STATE → THE PRODUCT TEST FAILS
The learner should understand that two qubits have joint outcomes, and that a joint table can break the rule that independent qubits follow.
The key conceptual transition is:
“Two qubits have four joint outcomes. If they were independent, the joint probabilities would multiply. The Bell state's do not, so entanglement is not two independent superpositions.”
This section should prepare the learner for Quantum Circuits (02.06).
________________________________________
Learning Outcomes
By the end of the section, the learner should be able to:
	List the four joint outcomes of two qubits, 00, 01, 10, and 11, and state the order convention: q0 first, and the control.
	Read a joint probability table, and read each qubit's own probabilities off it.
	Explain that for independent qubits the joint probability is the product of the separate probabilities, and compute the joint table of two independent qubits.
	Describe CNOT as a conditional flip with a control and a target, and trace it on basis states with a truth table.
	Explain that CNOT acts on each part of a superposition separately.
	Produce the Bell state's outcome table from |00⟩ with H on q0 and then CNOT: 00 and 11 each 50%.
	Apply the product test and explain why the Bell outcomes cannot come from two independent qubits.
	Explain that entanglement is not two independent superpositions, using |+⟩ and |+⟩ against the Bell state: each qubit alone is 50/50 in both.
	State that neither qubit of an entangled pair has a state of its own to draw on the sphere, and that some software libraries order bit strings differently.
	Ask how these steps can be written down as a diagram.

Page Structure
1. From One Qubit to Two
Begin directly from the Quantum Gates page.
Revisit the familiar progression:
ONE QUBIT'S STATE CAN BE MOVED AND UNDONE
↓
TWO QUBITS HAVE BEEN SHOWN SIDE BY SIDE, WITH NOTHING LINKING THEM
Then ask:
“What changes when there is more than one qubit?”
Name Module 01's Bell pair (each result random alone, the two always agreeing) and the CHSH game. Say that this page computes the pair from joint outcomes.
Do not repeat the CHSH game or its numbers.
The learner should see two qubits as a system with joint outcomes rather than as two separate one-qubit pictures.

2. Joint Outcomes
Introduce the four joint outcomes of two qubits: 00, 01, 10, and 11.
Clearly distinguish:
	Joint outcome — what both qubits show together.
	Own probabilities — what one qubit shows, read off the joint table.
State the convention in one box: the first qubit, q0, is written first (leftmost), is the CNOT control, and is the top wire. The left digit of an outcome belongs to q0.
Add one sentence that some software libraries write bit strings with qubit 0 on the right, so check the documentation of any tool before reading a bit string. Do not name or characterize a particular vendor without checking its current documentation.
Show a joint probability table, and read each qubit's own probabilities off it by adding along rows and columns.
Do not imply that the order is universal.
Include a short explanation such as:
“Why does 01 not mean the second qubit first?”
“On this page the left digit is q0 and the right digit is q1. Another tool may write them the other way round.”

3. Independent Qubits
Reconnect to 02.04's two qubits side by side.
Introduce the product rule: if the qubits are independent, the joint probability is the product of the separate probabilities.
The learner should understand:
INDEPENDENT QUBITS
↓
EACH JOINT PROBABILITY IS THE PRODUCT OF THE TWO SEPARATE ONES
Work two examples:
	q0 in |+⟩ and q1 in |0⟩: 00 is 50%, 10 is 50%, and 01 and 11 never happen.
	Both in |+⟩: every outcome is 25%.
Show the product rule as a small table.
Say that the rule holds for independent qubits. Do not claim that matching the rule proves the qubits are independent.
Do not require vectors or tensor products at Beginner level.

4. CNOT: A Conditional Flip
Introduce CNOT as a two-qubit gate with a control and a target.
Clearly distinguish:
	Control — the qubit whose value decides whether the flip happens. This is q0.
	Target — the qubit that is flipped when the control is 1. This is q1.
Show a truth table on basis states: 00 stays 00, 01 stays 01, 10 becomes 11, and 11 becomes 10.
Show the symbol through QedCircuit: a dot on the control wire and a ⊕ on the target wire.
Say that CNOT acts on each part of a superposition separately: the part with the control at 0 is untouched, and the part with the control at 1 has its target flipped.
Say that CNOT can be undone, as every gate can.
Do not imply that CNOT copies a qubit or reads the control.
Do not show a matrix at Beginner level.
Include a short explanation such as:
“Does CNOT look at the control?”
“No. It flips the target on the part of the state where the control is 1, and leaves the rest alone.”

5. Main Interactive — “Two-Qubit Explorer”
Make this the primary V1 interaction.
Allow the learner to choose two single-qubit inputs and optionally apply H to q0 and then CNOT, and watch the joint probability table.
At minimum support:
	An input for q0 and an input for q1: |0⟩, |1⟩, or |+⟩, taken from QedQubit.PRESETS.
	A toggle “Apply H to q0”.
	A toggle “Apply CNOT (q0 control, q1 target)”, applied after H.
	A switch for the product test panel.
	Resetting the view to |0⟩ and |0⟩ with both toggles off.
When the learner changes an input or a toggle:
	Show the steps as a two-wire circuit through QedCircuit, with the inputs named beside the wires.
	Show the four joint probability bars in the order 00, 01, 10, 11 through QedShots.renderBars, each with a percentage.
	Show the four amplitudes as a plain labelled list of real numbers, for listing only.
	Show each qubit's own probabilities.
	Show a one-line caption in words, such as “H made q0 an even mix. CNOT then copied the choice onto q1, so 00 and 11 are the only outcomes.”
	Show the circuit, the bars, the amplitudes, and the own probabilities in separate places.
When the product test panel is on:
	Show the actual 2×2 table with the row total on the right and the column total at the bottom.
	Show beside it the table that independent qubits with the same own probabilities would give.
	Show a one-sentence verdict in words and with an icon: “These match what independent qubits would give”, or “These do not match what independent qubits would give, so the qubits are not independent”.
	Never claim independence from a match alone.
The learner should visually experience:
CHOOSE THE INPUTS AND THE STEPS
↓
SEE THE JOINT OUTCOMES
↓
TEST THEM AGAINST WHAT INDEPENDENT QUBITS WOULD GIVE
Reinforce the distinction established on 02.01 to 02.04:
A state is what was prepared; the table is what it would give, never a measurement result.
Make the interaction resettable and allow learners to experiment with every input and both toggles.
At Intermediate and above, as a level-aware behavior gated through QedLevel:
	Add |−⟩, |+i⟩, and |−i⟩ as inputs.
	Show the four-component vector, with complex entries written as on 02.02 and 02.04.
	Add a matrix panel with the 4×4 matrix of the step, the input vector, and the output vector.
	Show the number αδ − βγ, with a note on what zero means.
The Beginner behavior must never depend on the level.
Make the interaction keyboard operable. Give the circuit a text description, pair every bar with a text percentage, and summarize each change, including the verdict, in an aria-live region.
Do not add shots or sampling, Bloch spheres, a free chain of gates, a third qubit, or another two-qubit gate here.

6. The Bell State
Start from |00⟩, apply H to q0, and then CNOT. Use the explorer.
Show the outcome table: 00 and 11 at 50%, and 01 and 10 never.
Introduce the term:
Bell state — the pair that H then CNOT produces from |00⟩.
Apply the product test. Each qubit alone is 50/50, so independent qubits would give 25% for every outcome, but the table has 50%, 0%, 0%, and 50%.
The learner should discover that no pair of independent qubits can give this table.
Contrast with |+⟩ and |+⟩. Each qubit alone is also 50/50, and the joint table does match the product.
Ask:
“Both pairs have a 50/50 qubit on each side. What is different about the Bell state?”
Answer: its joint outcomes always agree, so the qubits are not independent. Entanglement is not two independent superpositions.
Say that neither qubit of the Bell state has a state of its own to draw on the sphere. This is the limit that the Bloch Sphere page named.
Connect this directly to Module 01: the pair that was random alone and always agreeing is this table.
Include three checks with immediate explanatory feedback that explains every wrong choice:
	A prediction task: q0 in |+⟩ and q1 in |0⟩, then CNOT. Predict the joint probabilities (50% for 00 and 50% for 11), then compare with the bars. The feedback names the Bell state and notes that the qubits are now linked.
	A conceptual question: which joint tables could come from two independent qubits? 25% for every outcome could, and 50% for 00 with 50% for 10 could. 50% for 00 with 50% for 11 could not, and neither could 50% for 01 with 50% for 10. The feedback explains each with the product rule.
	A transfer task: make 01 and 10 each happen 50% of the time, with 00 and 11 never. Start with q0 in |0⟩ and q1 in |1⟩, apply H to q0, then CNOT. The feedback notes that the outcomes are always opposite and that the product test fails here too.
Do not teach how to measure the pair, the CHSH game, or the “no communication” account.

7. Beyond the Core
Build the heading inside the Intermediate block, as on 02.01 to 02.04, so a Beginner sees neither an empty heading nor a table-of-contents entry for it.
[Intermediate] Tensor Products and Four-Component Vectors
Add a gated block that covers:
	The product of two states as a four-component vector in the order 00, 01, 10, 11.
	|+⟩⊗|0⟩ = (|00⟩ + |10⟩)/√2 as a worked example.
	H on q0 as H⊗I, with the note that the first factor is q0.
	The reading-order reminder from 02.04: a circuit reads left to right, and a matrix product reads right to left.
[Intermediate] CNOT as a Matrix, the Bell State, and Separability
Add a second gated block after the first, covering:
	The 4×4 CNOT matrix in the basis order 00, 01, 10, 11, with q0 as the control.
	Its action on a general state: α|00⟩ + β|01⟩ + γ|10⟩ + δ|11⟩ goes to α|00⟩ + β|01⟩ + δ|10⟩ + γ|11⟩.
	Deriving the Bell state step by step: H on q0 gives (|00⟩ + |10⟩)/√2, and CNOT gives (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2.
	The separability test: a two-qubit state is a product exactly when αδ = βγ.
	Three worked cases: the Bell state (αδ ≠ βγ), |+⟩⊗|+⟩ (αδ = βγ), and the entangled state with amplitudes ½, ½, ½, −½, which gives 25% for every outcome and still fails the test.
Explain the ideas visually, through the explorer's vector and matrix panel, rather than relying on the equations.
Do not state the general Born rule. It belongs to 02.07.
Do not teach three or more qubits or other named two-qubit gates here.
[Advanced] Naming the Formalism
Add a very short gated block naming the Schmidt decomposition, entanglement measures, and the general controlled-U gate, with no formalism.
Build all three as gated blocks as described in @llm/CONVENTIONS.md section 7.

8. From Two Qubits to Circuits
End by comparing what the learner can do now with what is still hard.
So far:
DESCRIBE TWO QUBITS BY THEIR JOINT OUTCOMES
↓
LINK THEM WITH CNOT
↓
MAKE THE BELL STATE
versus:
WRITE THE STEPS DOWN IN ONE PLACE
↓
READ SOMEONE ELSE'S STEPS
↓
TRACE WHAT EACH STEP DOES
Present a final example with the explorer's two-wire drawing of H then CNOT, with no measurement symbol.
Ask:
“How can we write these steps down as a diagram?”
Then introduce the next problem:
The steps are described in words and tables. We need a standard way to draw them that anyone can read.
Do not explain circuit conventions here.
End with:
“How can we write these steps down as a diagram?”

Important Pedagogical Constraints
Do not imply:
ENTANGLEMENT = TWO INDEPENDENT SUPERPOSITIONS
Instead teach:
|+⟩ and |+⟩ are two independent superpositions, and the Bell state is not. Its joint outcomes do not multiply.
Do not imply:
PASSING THE PRODUCT TEST PROVES INDEPENDENCE
Instead teach:
Failing the product test proves the qubits are not independent. Passing it alone does not settle the question.
Do not imply:
THE BELL STATE = TWO QUBITS THAT HAPPEN TO AGREE
Instead teach:
The qubits share one state whose joint outcomes always agree. How the correlations are measured, and why they are not a hidden agreed answer, belongs to the measurement page and Module 01.
Do not imply that CNOT copies a qubit or reads the control. It flips the target on the part of the state where the control is 1, and it can be undone.
Do not imply that each qubit of an entangled pair has its own state. Neither has one to draw on the sphere.
Do not imply that the qubit order is universal, or that 01 means the same qubit in every tool.
Do not imply that this page shows how to measure the pair. The tables are theoretical probabilities, and sampled counts belong to 02.07.
Do not teach the CHSH game, shots and histograms, the Born rule as a formula, the circuit builder, three or more qubits, other named two-qubit gates, stabilizers, syndromes, noise, or decoherence here.
Keep Beginner V1 focused on the mental model required to understand why steps on two qubits need a standard way to be written down.
