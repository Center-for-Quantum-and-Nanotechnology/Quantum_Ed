________________________________________
Goal
Teach the learner that a physical qubit is two chosen states of a real object, and that the object has to be able to start in a known state, be controlled, be connected to others, be read, and keep its state, which Module 02’s ideal qubit got for free.
The central progression is:
THE IDEAL QUBIT → A REAL OBJECT WITH MANY LEVELS → TWO LEVELS CHOSEN → FIVE NEEDS → START IN A KNOWN STATE → BE CONTROLLABLE → TO BE CONTINUED
The learner should understand that making something into a qubit is a matter of arranging a real object so that two of its states can be started, controlled, connected, read, and kept, and that each of those takes something from the object.
The key conceptual transition is:
“Module 02’s qubit started in |0⟩, rotated exactly, and was read perfectly because it was an idea. A real qubit is two levels of a real object, and every one of those has to be arranged.”
This section should prepare the learner for Gates and Control (04.01.02).
________________________________________
Learning Outcomes
By the end of the section, the learner should be able to:
	Say what Module 02’s ideal qubit gets for free: a perfect start, exact operations, perfect readout, unlimited time, and no outside influence.
	Define a physical qubit as two chosen states of a real object, not the whole object.
	Describe an object’s levels as a ladder of distinct energies, and say that a qubit uses two of them.
	Say that the ladder is one picture, and that some qubits, such as a photon’s polarization, use two states that are not levels of different energy.
	Distinguish natural from engineered qubits, and say what that implies for how alike two qubits are.
	Name the five needs in plain words: start, control, connect, read, and keep.
	Explain why a qubit has to start in a known state, and how the size of the gap and the temperature decide how reliably it does.
	Explain why a drive has to single out the qubit’s two levels, and what goes wrong when the steps between levels are all the same size.
	Say that every way into a qubit is also a way for disturbance to get in.
	Distinguish a physical qubit from the ideal qubit and from a logical qubit.
	Say that passing one or two of the needs does not make something a good qubit.
	Ask how the state of a physical qubit is changed on purpose.

Page Structure
1. What the Ideal Qubit Got for Free
Begin directly from the Module 02 material.
Revisit what Module 02 let the qubit do:
	Start in |0⟩.
	Change state exactly as a gate says.
	Report its state truthfully when measured.
	Last as long as you like.
	Be untouched by anything else.
Present this as a short table with one row per item. A third column, filled in at the end of the page, will say what the real object has to provide and which page takes it up.
Then ask:
“What does a real object have to do to be a qubit?”
Define physical qubit in one sentence, in the glossary’s words: a real object, or part of one, that is used as a qubit.
The learner should see this page as asking what Module 02 took for granted, not as a new kind of qubit.

2. Many Levels, Two Chosen
Introduce the energy ladder with a small static diagram.
A real object can be in one of several distinct levels, each with its own energy. Show five.
A qubit uses two of them. Label them |0⟩ and |1⟩.
Mark the other levels “outside the qubit”. They are still there.
Say that the ladder is the picture for atoms, ions, and engineered circuits.
Say once that not every qubit is made of levels of different energy. A photon’s two polarizations have the same energy, and 04.02.04 shows them.
State the general requirement in plain words:
Two states of a real object that we can tell apart, start in, and control.
Say that the system on this page is invented, and that its units are invented.
Do not draw the ladder as a ball on steps or as places in space.
Include a short explanation such as:
“A qubit is not the whole atom or circuit. It is the two levels we choose to use.”

3. Natural and Engineered Qubits
Use two short cards, not an interaction.
Natural
An atom or an ion. Nature sets its levels, so every one of the same kind is alike.
Engineered
A circuit built so that it behaves like an atom, sometimes called an artificial atom. Its levels depend on how it was made, so no two come out exactly alike.
Give one consequence for each. Identical ones are easy to compare. Engineered ones have to be measured and tuned one at a time, which 04.01.05 returns to.
Name superconducting circuits, trapped ions, and neutral atoms as examples, without teaching them.
Do not imply that engineered means less real, or that natural means better.
Do not show any platform detail or device picture. Module 04’s platform pages own them.

4. Five Needs
Introduce the five needs in plain words, each with one sentence:
	Start — put it in a known state.
	Control — change its state on purpose.
	Connect — let qubits affect each other when we want.
	Read — find out what state it is in.
	Keep — its state lasts long enough to use.
Say which page takes up each: this page and 04.01.02 (start, control), 04.01.02 and the platform pages (connect), 04.01.03 (read), 04.01.04 and 04.01.05 (keep).
Use the same words for the needs here and in the Platform Card, so the module has one vocabulary.
Say that this page tests the first two.
Ask:
“Which of these did Module 02 let you ignore?”

5. Main Interactive — “Choose a Qubit”
Make this the primary V1 interaction.
Allow the learner to change the properties of an invented five-level system and see whether it can start and be controlled.
At minimum support:
	Spacing: Evenly spaced; Slightly uneven (each step 2% smaller than the one before); Clearly uneven (each step 10% smaller than the one before).
	Gap, the first step: Small (1); Medium (2); Large (4). Invented units.
	Temperature: Warm (4); Cool (1); Cold (0.25). Invented units.
	Reset, which returns to Evenly spaced, Medium, Warm. That starting setting fails both tests, so the learner has something to fix.
The qubit is always the two lowest levels. Say that this is a choice of this teaching model, that other choices of levels exist, and that the next two subsections give the reasons it is the natural one here.
When the learner changes a setting:
	Show the system: the ladder with the two qubit levels marked, the other levels marked “outside the qubit”, the steps labelled with their sizes, and the heat shown as a labelled marker.
	Show the starting shares: the share of starts that begin in |0⟩, in |1⟩, and in a level outside the qubit, as bars with percentages and a sentence. Reuse QedShots.renderBars.
	Show the response to a drive tuned to the first step: for each other step, whether it responds (its size is within 5% of the first step’s size) or does not, with its difference from the first step, as text and a glyph.
	Show the verdict: the five needs. Start passes when the share in |0⟩ is at least 99%. Control passes when no other step responds. Connect, Read, and Keep are shown as “not tested on this page”, with the page that takes each up. Never show them as passed.
	End the verdict with one sentence: passing two needs is not being a qubit.
Keep what the learner changes (the settings) separate from what is derived (the shares and the per-step responses) and from what is observed (the verdict).
The learner should visually experience:
SPACING, GAP, AND TEMPERATURE
↓
WHERE THE SYSTEM STARTS, AND WHICH STEPS A DRIVE WOULD MOVE
↓
WHETHER IT CAN START AND BE CONTROLLED
Reinforce the distinction established in Module 02:
A qubit is an idea with two states. A physical qubit is a real object arranged to provide them.
Make the interaction resettable and keyboard accessible. Controls are radio groups. Give the ladder a text description. Present the results as a real table, with stacked rows on a phone. Announce the new shares and which steps respond in an aria-live region when a setting changes. Keep focus where it was after the view re-renders.
Label the system invented, and label the Start and Control results as computed from a simplified model.
Include these checks:
	Prediction task: “You keep the gap the same and make the system colder. Does the share that starts in |0⟩ go up, go down, or stay the same?” The answer is up. Then compare with the explorer.
	Conceptual question: “Is a physical qubit the whole atom or circuit?” Give feedback on each wrong choice: it is the two levels chosen to stand for 0 and 1, and the other levels are still there.
	Transfer task: “A designer builds a system whose steps are all the same size. It starts reliably when cold. Why is it still a poor qubit?” The answer is that a drive tuned to the first step would move the system between the other levels too, so it cannot be controlled as a qubit. Then ask which need it failed. The answer is control.
Do not add a drive timing, a rotation angle, a readout, a lifetime, a kind of noise, a platform, or a real number.

6. Starting in a Known State
Explain what the first result shows.
A system left alone settles toward its lowest levels. Heat jostles it back up.
The qubit starts reliably when the gap is large compared with the heat. Cooling is one way to get a known start. Others, such as using light to push a system into a chosen level, are named on the platform pages.
Connect this to 02.01: the recipe is the same each time, and the real result is a share, not a certainty.
Say that this is why some devices are made very cold. Do not say that every device must be.
Keep it to words and the bars. Do not show a formula.
Do not imply that the start is perfect.

7. Being Controllable: Singling Out Two Levels
Explain what the second result shows.
A drive is tuned to the step between the two lowest levels.
If other steps are the same size, the same drive moves the system there too, and it can leave the qubit’s two levels. This is called leakage, and 04.01.05 returns to it.
Unevenly spaced levels let the drive single out the pair. This is why a good qubit is built or chosen so that its levels are not evenly spaced.
Say the 5% tolerance is invented, and that real devices differ.
Say that the page has not yet said how the drive works or how large a rotation is. That is 04.01.02.
Do not use the word anharmonic at Beginner. “Unevenly spaced” carries the idea.

8. Isolated, Yet Reachable
Use a short callout with a two-sided diagram, not an interaction.
A qubit has to be cut off from its surroundings to keep its state.
It also has to be connected to the outside, so that we can start, control, and read it.
Every connection is also a way for disturbance to get in.
Say that this trade-off returns on every page of the module.
Add one sentence on the Module 07 fence: a physical qubit is one such object. Qubits made from many of them are taught in Module 07.
Do not name sources of noise or give lifetimes.

9. From a Qubit That Starts to a Qubit That Moves
End by returning to the table from subsection 1 and filling in its third column: what the real object has to provide, and the page that takes it up.
Previously:
THE IDEAL QUBIT
↓
EVERYTHING FREE
versus:
A PHYSICAL QUBIT
↓
EVERYTHING ARRANGED
Present the closing question:
“You have a qubit that starts in |0⟩ and a drive that can single out its two levels. How exactly do you move its state, and how far?”
Then introduce the next problem:
Changing a physical qubit’s state is a physical act with a size and a duration.
Do not explain pulses or rotation angles here.
End with:
“How does a signal turn a qubit’s state?”

Important Pedagogical Constraints
Do not imply:
A PHYSICAL QUBIT = AN ATOM, ION, OR CIRCUIT
Instead teach:
It is two chosen states of one. The other levels are still there.
Do not imply:
EVERY QUBIT = THE LOWEST TWO LEVELS OF A LADDER
Instead teach:
The ladder is one picture. A photon’s polarization is a counterexample. The general requirement is two states we can tell apart, start in, and control.
Do not imply:
START + CONTROL = A GOOD QUBIT
Instead teach:
Connect, read, and keep are not tested here. Passing two needs is not being a qubit.
Do not imply that a device must be cold. Cooling is one way to start in a known state.
Do not imply that the start is perfect. It is a share, not a certainty.
Do not imply that engineered qubits are less real, or that natural ones are better.
Do not imply that the page’s numbers are real device values. They are invented and labelled.
Do not imply that a physical qubit is a logical qubit.
Do not imply that the qubit is a ball on a ladder, or that a level is a place in space.
Do not imply that passing the explorer’s checks is how real qubits are chosen. It is a simplified model of two of the reasons.
Do not teach gates or rotation angles, readout, lifetimes, noise kinds, any one platform, error correction, how software describes a device, or real numbers here.
Keep Beginner V1 focused on the mental model required to understand why a physical qubit has to be controlled.
