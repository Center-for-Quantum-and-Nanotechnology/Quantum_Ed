Goal
Teach the learner that an operation goes wrong for several different reasons at once, that a source of noise is not the same as the error it causes or the figure a device reports, that sources differ from qubit to qubit and settings drift with time, and that this is why a device is measured and tuned again and again.
The central progression is:
A QUBIT YOU CAN START, TURN, READ, AND KEEP → AN OPERATION THAT STILL GOES WRONG → FIVE KINDS OF SOURCE → SOURCE ≠ ERROR ≠ REPORTED FIGURE → QUBITS THAT DIFFER → A SETTING THAT DRIFTS → MEASURE, SET, USE, DRIFT, MEASURE AGAIN → TO BE CONTINUED
The learner should understand that Module 02’s operations were exact and its numbers were certain because they were ideas, and that on a device several sources disturb every operation, each qubit differs, the settings drift, and the figure a device reports is one measured number for all of it.
The key conceptual transition is:
“Module 02’s operations were exact and its numbers were certain. On a device, several sources disturb every operation, each qubit differs, the settings drift, and the figure a device reports is one measured number for all of it.”
This section should prepare the learner for Superconducting Qubits (04.02.01), by asking how different physical systems answer the same questions.
________________________________________
Learning Outcomes
By the end of the section, the learner should be able to:
	Say that an operation goes wrong for several reasons at once.
	Name five kinds of source (the environment, the control, the neighbours, leakage, and the readout), and say in one line what each is.
	Distinguish a noise source from an error and from a reported figure, with an example of each.
	Read an error budget and say which source is largest for an operation.
	Say that the largest source changes with the qubit, the operation, and the time.
	Say that the reported figure is one number for all the sources together, and cannot say which one dominated.
	Say that a real device cannot switch a source off, and how it finds out which dominates.
	Say that qubits on one device differ from one another, and that their numbers change.
	Say that a setting drifts over time, and that the drift on this page is a teaching model with no universal rate.
	Describe the loop of measure, set, use, drift, and measure again, and name the three experiments it re-runs.
	Say that a tune is itself a measurement and leaves a small residual.
	Say that a reported figure is a measurement at one moment and not a guarantee, and ask how different physical systems answer these questions.

Page Structure
1. From Keeping to Everything Else
Begin directly from the Coherence material.
Revisit the familiar progression:
A QUBIT THAT STARTS
↓
A SIGNAL THAT TURNS IT
↓
A READOUT THAT SORTS ITS SIGNAL
↓
A STATE THAT FADES, AT A KNOWN SPEED
Then ask 04.01.04’s closing question:
“Where does the rest of the imperfection come from?”
Answer in one sentence: from several different sources at once, and they do not stand still.
The learner should see this page as the physical side of Module 02’s assumption that operations are exact.

2. Five Kinds of Source
Show a short table of five, one line each, with where the learner has already seen its effect:
	The environment — the qubit is never perfectly cut off, so it loses energy and phase (04.01.04).
	The control — the signal is never exactly the one set, and its strength drifts (04.01.02).
	The neighbours — an operation on one qubit disturbs others. This is crosstalk, the unintended interaction, and it is different from the intended coupling of 04.01.02.
	Leakage — the qubit leaves its two levels and ends in a level outside them (04.01.01).
	The readout — the signals overlap, so a read can be wrong (04.01.03).
Say that noise is the general word for all of them together. Say that the spread of the readout signal in 04.01.03 and the loss of energy and phase in 04.01.04 are two examples.
Do not name materials, causes, or spectra. Do not show a map of qubits or a crosstalk matrix.

3. Source, Error, Reported Figure
Show a three-line table, each line with an example from the explorer:
	A source — what disturbs. For example, the environment.
	An error — the share of runs of this operation that go wrong because of that source. For example, 1.2% of the runs.
	A reported figure — what a device measures by repeating the operation and counting how often it went right. For example, its gate fidelity, or for a readout, its readout fidelity.
Use numbers from the model for the examples, not typed numbers.
Say that these are three different things, and that Module 07 reuses the distinction.
Name Module 07’s words once: “Module 07 has names for the kinds of error these sources cause (bit-flip, phase-flip, Pauli, and depolarizing are among them) and models that put numbers on them. This page does not teach them.” Link to the existing glossary entry for Noise Model once.
Say once that “reported figure” is the “reported metric” of Module 05.
Do not use “channel”, any model, or any error vocabulary beyond that one sentence.

4. Main Interactive — “Error Budget”
Make this the primary V1 interaction.
Allow the learner to pick a qubit and an operation, switch the sources on and off, move time forward, and see which source is largest.
At minimum support:
	Qubit: A, B, or C, the three invented qubits of 04.01.04, with the T1 and T2 they already have and three more invented differences.
	Operation: the one-qubit signal, the two-qubit gate, or the readout, the Bell circuit’s three operations, with the invented lengths 1, 4, and 10 from the 04.01.02 timeline.
	Sources: five checkboxes (Environment, Control, Neighbours, Leakage, Readout), all on at the start. A source that does not apply to the chosen operation is shown as “not applicable” and never hidden.
	Time: a slider for the time now, 0 to 48 in invented time steps, with a typed value, clamped. Default 0.
	Measure the reported figure, which runs 1,000 simulated repeats of the operation.
	Recalibrate, which re-runs the drive-and-flip experiment and stores a new half-turn length.
	Reset, which returns to qubit A, the one-qubit signal, all sources on, time 0, and freshly tuned.
Each source is the error share of the chosen operation: the share of its runs that go wrong because of that source.
	Environment: from QedCoherence. The energy loss and phase loss over the operation’s length, from the qubit’s T1 and T2. For a gate it is the average of the two. For a readout only energy loss matters, and only for a 1, so it is half of the energy loss. Applies to all three operations.
	Control: from QedDrive. The half turn’s offset between the strength now in force and the half-turn length stored at the last tune, as the chance that the qubit does not end in |1⟩. The two-qubit gate is twice as sensitive to the same offset (an invented factor, labelled). Not applicable to the readout.
	Neighbours: invented shares for each qubit and operation, standing for crosstalk. Applies to all three.
	Leakage: invented shares for each qubit and operation, standing for a qubit ending in a level outside its two. Applies to the two gates. Not applicable to the readout.
	Readout: from QedReadout. The average of the two misreads at the middle threshold for Medium signals. Applies to the readout only.
The invented numbers are fixed:
	Drift of the strength per time step: A +0.1%, B −0.3%, C +0.2%.
	Neighbours, for the one-qubit signal, the two-qubit gate, and the readout: A 0.10%, 1.6%, 0.3%. B 0.30%, 1.0%, 0.4%. C 0.20%, 0.8%, 0.5%.
	Leakage, for the one-qubit signal and the two-qubit gate: A 0.40%, 0.6%. B 0.10%, 0.3%. C 0.15%, 0.2%.
The shares add when they are small. The model computes the exact combination (one minus the product of the chances of going right), and the page says “added up”. Show no formula.
Design rule: every source is the largest somewhere in the grid of qubit, operation, and time, and none is the largest everywhere. The numbers above are chosen so that this holds, and a test pins it.
When the learner changes a setting:
	Show the settings: the controls, the time now, and the time of the last tune.
	Show the model’s exact answer, tagged “computed from a simplified model”:
		One labelled row for each source with a bar and its error share, drawn instead of a stacked bar so that no source depends on a color to be told apart. Reuse QedShots.renderBars.
		The total of the sources that are on, and the word “largest” with a glyph on the largest.
		The drift plot of the control error against the time since the last tune, with a gray “tuned” tick at each tune.
	Show the observation, only after the learner asks for it, tagged “simulated”:
		After Measure the reported figure, the share of 1,000 runs that went right, in the telemetry treatment (teal, dashed border, labelled “measured by repeating the operation”). Then state the model’s own value and say that the figure moves from run to run. Say that the reported figure is one number and cannot say which source dominated.
		After Recalibrate, the length found, the length it replaced, and the offset left over.
Say plainly that switching a source off is something only this model can do. A real device cannot remove a source, so it finds out which dominates by running more experiments. Name randomized benchmarking as a method, and say it is Intermediate.
Recalibrate re-runs the drive-and-flip experiment from 04.01.02 (the seeded sweep over 25 lengths, 100 shots each) at the strength now in force, takes the middle of the lengths with the most 1s, stores it as the new half-turn length, and sets the time of the last tune to now. The control error then falls, but not to zero: the peak is flat, there are only 100 shots, and the lengths are 0.5 apart, so the tune is itself a measurement with a residual. The first state of the page is a tune at time 0, computed the same way.
Keep what the learner changes (the qubit, the operation, the sources, the time) separate from what is derived (the error shares and the drift plot) and from what is observed (the reported figure and the result of a tune).
The learner should visually experience:
QUBIT, OPERATION, SOURCES, AND TIME
↓
THE ERROR SHARE OF EACH SOURCE, AND WHICH IS LARGEST
↓
WHAT A MEASUREMENT WOULD REPORT
Reinforce the distinction established in the taxonomy:
Noise source ≠ error ≠ reported figure.
Make the interaction resettable and keyboard accessible. Controls are native checkboxes and radio groups in option pills, a slider with a typed value, and real buttons. Give the budget rows a text summary, and put the data in a table inside a closed details element (the five shares, the total, and the drift curve’s values at a few times). Announce the new total and the largest source in an aria-live region when a setting changes, and the result after a measure or a tune. Keep focus where it was after the view re-renders. Present tables as real tables, with stacked rows on a phone.
Include these checks:
	Prediction task: “You move time forward without recalibrating. Does the control error for the two-qubit gate on Qubit B go up, go down, or stay the same?” The answer is up. Then compare with the explorer.
	Conceptual question: “A device reports 99% for an operation. Does that tell you which source caused the other 1%?” Give feedback on each wrong choice: it is one number for all the sources together, and finding which dominates takes more experiments.
	Transfer task: “A device was tuned this morning. This afternoon its two-qubit gate is worse, but its T1 has not changed. What would you suspect, and what would you do?” The answer is that a setting has drifted, probably the control, so measure it again and set it again. Then ask: “Does re-tuning make the error zero?” The answer is no: the tune is a measurement and leaves a residual.
Do not add a platform, real numbers, a schedule, how software behaves, a noise model, or error vocabulary beyond the one naming sentence.

5. Why Qubits Differ
Show a generated table of the three invented qubits and what differs: T1 and T2, the drift pace and direction, the neighbours’ share, and the leakage share.
Say that engineered qubits are never exactly alike (Physical Qubits, 04.01.01), and that real qubits on one device differ from one another and their numbers change.
Say that a software layer (Module 05) is told the differences.
Make no claim about how much, or about any platform.

6. Why a Device Drifts, and the Loop
Explain what the explorer showed: a setting shifts slowly, so the offset since the last tune grows, so the device measures it again.
Say what the learner can know about why: the settings of the control equipment and the qubit’s own properties shift slowly with their surroundings, which nobody holds perfectly still. Name no cause.
Say that the drift in the explorer is a teaching model. Real drift is not steady, depends on the platform and the setting, and has no universal rate, as Hardware-Aware QEC also says.
Describe the loop in five words:
MEASURE
↓
SET
↓
USE
↓
DRIFT
↓
MEASURE AGAIN
Show a three-row table of what a device re-measures:
	The half-turn length — the drive-and-flip experiment — Gates and Control.
	The readout threshold — the threshold sweep — Readout.
	T1 and T2 — the wait-and-read experiment — Coherence.
Say that a tune is itself a measurement with a residual.
Say that a dated report of such values is a calibration snapshot, and that what software does with it is Module 05.
Do not give a schedule for how often a device does this.
Do not describe how software responds.

7. A Number Is Not a Guarantee
Explain that the reported figure is one number for all the sources, that it comes from a count and moves from run to run, and that it describes one moment.
Say that a metric is not a guarantee, which Hardware Abstraction (05.04) also says.
Name randomized benchmarking as a method, Intermediate.
Ask:
“Two devices report the same fidelity. Do they fail in the same way?”
Answer no: their budgets can differ.
Do not teach how any figure is extracted.

8. What Fundamentals Added Up To
Show a recap table of the five needs of Physical Qubits (start, control, connect, read, and keep), each with how it goes wrong and the page that showed it, in the order and wording of the earlier recaps.
Say that the one-qubit thread (start, drive, wait, read) is now complete, and that the Bell circuit’s three operations carry the thread from here.
Do not introduce a platform.

9. From One Qubit to Different Qubits
End by comparing what Module 02 assumed with what this page showed.
Show a short table of five things, each against the Module 02 assumption, in the same order and wording as the earlier recaps:
	The operation — it was exact.
	The errors — there were none.
	The numbers — they were certain.
	The settings — they were fixed.
	The qubits — they were all alike.
Previously:
AN OPERATION
↓
EXACT
versus:
AN OPERATION
↓
SEVERAL SOURCES, ONE MEASURED FIGURE, SETTINGS THAT DRIFT, AND QUBITS THAT DIFFER
Present the closing question:
“A qubit has to be started, controlled, connected, read, and kept. Each of these goes wrong in its own way, and each setting drifts. But the answers depend on what the qubit is made of. How do different physical systems answer?”
Then introduce the next problem:
The same five needs, answered by different physical systems, with different trade-offs.
Do not explain any platform here.
End with:
“How do different physical systems answer?”

Important Pedagogical Constraints
Do not imply:
A NOISE SOURCE = AN ERROR = A REPORTED FIGURE
Instead teach:
A source is what disturbs. An error is the share of runs that go wrong because of it. A reported figure is what a device measures for all the sources together.
Do not imply:
ONE SOURCE ALWAYS DOMINATES
Instead teach:
The largest source changes with the qubit, the operation, and the time, and the invented shares are not real.
Do not imply:
A TUNE MAKES THE ERROR ZERO
Instead teach:
A tune is a measurement and leaves a residual, and the setting drifts again.
Do not imply that a device can switch a source off. Only the model can.
Do not imply that the reported figure can say which source dominated.
Do not imply that drift is steady or has a universal rate. It is a teaching model.
Do not imply that the three qubits are real, or that qubits on one device are alike.
Do not imply that a reported figure is a guarantee. It is a measurement at one moment.
Do not imply that crosstalk and coupling are the same. One is intended, the other is not.
Do not teach any platform’s sources, the error vocabulary or noise models, how software responds to calibration, a recalibration schedule, or real numbers here.
Keep Beginner V1 focused on the mental model required to understand why the answers depend on what the qubit is made of.

End State
The learner should leave with:
AN OPERATION GOES WRONG FOR SEVERAL REASONS AT ONCE
↓
SOURCE ≠ ERROR ≠ REPORTED FIGURE
↓
QUBITS DIFFER, AND SETTINGS DRIFT
↓
MEASURE, SET, USE, DRIFT, MEASURE AGAIN
And understand the central relationship:
Sources → errors → one measured figure → a setting that drifts → a device that measures and sets it again.
The central takeaway should be:
Several sources disturb every operation, and a device reports one measured figure for all of them. Qubits differ, settings drift, and so a device is measured and tuned again and again, and no figure is a guarantee.
End with:
“How do different physical systems answer?”
