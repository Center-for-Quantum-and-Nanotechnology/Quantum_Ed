# 04.01.01 Physical Qubits — DRAFT

**Status:** Draft from taxonomy V1.0, for review. Not yet in the `section-spec-writer` format and not approved for building. After review, run the `section-spec-writer` skill over the reviewed draft to produce the final spec (same filename).
**Governing documents:** `taxonomy_QuantumHardware_v1.0.md` (row 04.01.01, sections 1, 3, 4 items 1 and 2, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, and decisions 5 to 9 and 12 to 14), `ProjectVision.md` (sections 8, 9, 12, 13, 18), `llm/CONVENTIONS.md` (sections 4, 7 experience levels, 8 tests), and the built Module 02 pages (especially 02.01 Quantum States, which owns preparation, and 02.07 Measurement) and the Module 07 page *Physical vs. Logical Qubits*, which already defines a physical qubit.
**Previous page:** none inside Module 04. The learner arrives from Module 02, whose closing note says real devices have imperfect readout, noise, and calibration. **Next page:** 04.01.02 Gates and Control.
**Weight of this page:** it is the first page of a module whose risk is turning into a physics course, and it has to teach the one physical idea the rest of the module depends on (a real object has distinct energy levels, and a qubit uses two of them) without teaching atomic physics. The draft keeps it small by making the **five needs** the spine of the page and the module, teaching **only two of them in depth** (starting in a known state, and being controllable), and handing the other three to the pages that own them. Three risks to watch:
- **The ladder as a false general model.** "Two levels of different energy" is true for atoms, ions, and circuits, and not true for a photon's polarization, whose two states have the same energy. The draft says the ladder is **one picture**, names the general requirement (two states we can tell apart and control), and keeps a counterexample in view (Open question 4).
- **Invented physics that looks real.** Temperatures, gaps, and percentages are **invented units**, labelled, with no real frequency, kelvin value, or device (taxonomy decision 6).
- **Duplicating Module 07.** *Physical vs. Logical Qubits* already defines a physical qubit and says each one is imperfect. This page does not teach logical qubits or encoding; it adds what a physical qubit has to be able to do.

**Why a new shared model is needed:** the page needs an energy ladder whose spacing, gap, and temperature the learner can change, with computed starting shares and a computed "can a drive pick out this pair?" check. No built module has this, and 04.01.02 to 04.01.05 reuse it (a drive tuned to a gap, levels told apart at readout, decay downward the ladder, and population leaking to a third level).

## Open questions for review

Each has a recommendation. Say which you accept or change; the rest of the draft already assumes the recommendations.

1. **What is the one idea the page teaches?** The taxonomy row says "what makes a physical system usable as a qubit and how it differs from Module 02's ideal one". *Recommendation:* **a physical qubit is two chosen states of a real object, and the object has to meet five needs that the ideal qubit got for free**: it must be able to **start** in a known state, be **controlled**, be **connected** to other qubits, be **read**, and **keep** its state long enough. The page teaches the idea and tests the first two needs. The other three are named, shown as not yet tested, and handed to 04.01.02 (control and connect), 04.01.03 (read), and 04.01.04 (keep). Use the same words for the needs here and in the Platform Card (taxonomy section 9), so the module has one vocabulary.

2. **What does the main interaction show, and how does it differ from the taxonomy's wording?** The taxonomy says "qubit chooser: pick two levels from an energy ladder and test the candidate against the five needs". *Recommendation:* a **Choose a Qubit** explorer on an **invented five-level system**, where the learner changes **three properties of the system** rather than picking which two levels to use: **spacing** (evenly spaced, slightly uneven, clearly uneven), **gap** (the first step: small, medium, large), and **temperature** (warm, cool, cold). The qubit is always the **two lowest levels**. *Why not let the learner pick the pair:* in a ladder like this one only the lowest pair can start by itself, so picking a pair is a guessing game with one right answer, and it teaches the false rule "always use the two lowest levels". Changing the properties produces real variation and real reasons. The page says other choices of levels exist and that this is a teaching model. The taxonomy's interaction text is a design intention, and this is a refinement of it; please confirm.

3. **What does the verdict test, and how is it kept honest?** *Recommendation:* the verdict lists the **five needs**. **Start** and **Control** are **computed** from the learner's settings and shown with a number or a yes/no and a sentence. **Connect**, **Read**, and **Keep** are shown as **"not tested on this page"** with the page that takes each up, never as a pass. This stops the page implying that a ladder that starts well and is selective is therefore a good qubit. A final line says exactly that: passing two needs is not being a qubit.

4. **How general is the energy-ladder picture, and what is said about it?** *Recommendation:* teach the ladder as the picture for **atoms, ions, and engineered circuits**, say once that **not every qubit is made of levels of different energy** (a photon's two polarizations have the same energy, and 04.02.04 shows them), and state the general requirement in plain words: **two states of a real object that we can tell apart, start in, and control**. Do not say "a qubit is the lowest two levels"; say "in this invented system, the two lowest levels are the natural pair, and here is why". Also say once that the two-level picture is an approximation, because the other levels are still there, which is what the Control check shows.

5. **How does "start in a known state" work, and how much temperature is shown?** *Recommendation:* the system **settles** into its lowest levels when left alone, and heat **jostles** it upward. The page shows, for a chosen gap and temperature, **the share of starts that begin in |0⟩, in |1⟩, and in a level outside the qubit**, as bars (reuse `QedShots.renderBars`) and as words ("almost always", "usually", "often the wrong level", with the thresholds stated). Computed in closed form from a thermal weighting over the five levels, labelled **illustrative**, in **invented units** (no kelvin, no frequency). The page makes three points: a bigger gap or a colder system starts more reliably; cooling is one way to get a known start (others, such as using light to push a system into a chosen level, are named on the platform pages); and the start is **never perfect**, which connects to 02.01's "same recipe, same state" (the recipe is the same, and the real result is a share, not a certainty). The Boltzmann formula is not shown at Beginner; it is an Intermediate "Show the math".

6. **How does the Control check work?** *Recommendation:* the learner sees the steps between neighbouring levels. A drive is **tuned to the first step** (|0⟩ to |1⟩). The check asks whether any **other** step is close enough in size that the same drive would also move the system there. The rule is stated: a step counts as "the same" if it is within an invented **tolerance** (5%) of the first. With **even** spacing every step is the same, so the drive can't single out the qubit; with **slightly uneven** spacing (2%) it is still too close; with **clearly uneven** spacing (10%) it is selective. The result per step is **text and a glyph** ("responds / does not respond"), not color. The consequence is named in one sentence ("the system can be pushed out of the two levels; this is called leakage and 04.01.05 returns to it"), without teaching leakage. Do not use the word *anharmonic* at Beginner; "unevenly spaced" carries the idea. The tolerance is invented, and the page says real devices differ.

7. **How are natural and engineered qubits shown?** *Recommendation:* two short cards, not an interaction. **Natural**: the same kind of atom or ion is the same everywhere, because nature set its levels (named only: atoms, ions). **Engineered**: a circuit is built so that it behaves like an atom, and its levels are set by how it is made, so no two come out exactly alike (the glossary's *artificial atom* idea). One consequence each: identical ones are easy to compare; engineered ones must be measured and tuned one by one, which 04.01.05 returns to. No platform detail and no pictures of devices; 04.02 owns them. Say that "engineered" does not mean "less real".

8. **How is "isolated yet reachable" taught?** *Recommendation:* a short **callout with a two-sided diagram**, no interaction: a qubit must be **cut off from its surroundings** to keep its state, and **connected to the outside** so that we can start, control, and read it, and **every connection is also a way for disturbance to get in**. The page does not name sources of noise (04.01.05) or lifetimes (04.01.04). This is the module's first appearance of the trade-off and it should be stated once and plainly.

9. **How is the Module 07 fence kept?** Module 07's *Physical vs. Logical Qubits* defines a physical qubit as a real, imperfect object and a logical qubit as a pattern across many. *Recommendation:* one sentence on this page: a physical qubit is **one** such object; later in the course you will meet qubits made from many of them, and that is taught in Module 07. No diagram, no encoding, no logical-qubit vocabulary beyond the name. The definition here agrees with the glossary's *Physical Qubit*; if the page needs a wording change, change the glossary entry rather than diverge.

10. **Where does the code live?** *Recommendation:* a **new pure module `QedLadder` (`ladder.js`)**. Data/state: the ladder as data (`levels(spacing, gap)`: energy, step to the next level, and whether each level belongs to the qubit), `SPACINGS`, `GAPS`, `TEMPERATURES` (the labelled invented values), `startShares(ladder, temperature)` (|0⟩, |1⟩, and other, summing to 1), `selectivity(ladder, tolerance)` (one result per other step, with its relative difference), `verdict(settings)` (the five needs, with Connect, Read, and Keep marked untested), and a words helper for the shares. Rendering: an SVG ladder view (levels as labelled bars, qubit levels distinguished, steps marked). The page wiring (three radio groups, Reset, the live summary) stays page-local. Reuse `QedShots.renderBars`, `QedNumbers.pct`, and `QedQuiz`. Reserved extension points for 04.01.02 to 04.01.05: a drive frequency, decay down the ladder, population in a third level, and level signals. See Shared Components.

11. **Experience levels.** *Recommendation:* as on Module 05, **V1 is Beginner-only, with no gated blocks.** Intermediate (future): energy-level diagrams with frequencies, the thermal weighting as a formula, anharmonicity and the transmon as the engineered answer, the two-level approximation and its limits, and the standard criteria (DiVincenzo's) named. ## Goal

Teach the learner that a physical qubit is two chosen states of a real object, and that the object has to be able to start in a known state, be controlled, be connected to others, be read, and keep its state, which the ideal qubit of Module 02 got for free.

Central progression:

`THE IDEAL QUBIT → A REAL OBJECT WITH MANY LEVELS → TWO LEVELS CHOSEN → FIVE NEEDS → START IN A KNOWN STATE → BE CONTROLLABLE → TO BE CONTINUED`

Key conceptual transition: **"Module 02's qubit started in |0⟩, rotated exactly, and was read perfectly because it was an idea. A real qubit is two levels of a real object, and every one of those has to be arranged."**

It opens by naming what Module 02 took for granted and ends by asking how the state is changed on purpose.

## Learning outcomes (Beginner)

The learner should be able to:

1. Say what Module 02's ideal qubit gets for free: a perfect start, exact operations, perfect readout, unlimited time, and no outside influence.
2. Define a physical qubit as two chosen states of a real object, not the whole object.
3. Describe the object's levels as a ladder of distinct energies, and say a qubit uses two of them.
4. Say that the ladder is one picture, and that some qubits (a photon's polarization, for example) use two states that are not levels of different energy.
5. Distinguish natural from engineered qubits, and say what that implies for how alike two qubits are.
6. Name the five needs in plain words: start, control, connect, read, and keep.
7. Explain why a qubit has to start in a known state, and how the gap and the temperature decide how reliably it does.
8. Explain why a drive has to single out the qubit's two levels, and what goes wrong when the steps between levels are all the same size.
9. Say that every way into a qubit is also a way for disturbance to get in.
10. Distinguish a physical qubit from the ideal qubit and from a logical qubit.
11. Say that passing one or two of the needs does not make something a good qubit.
12. Ask how the state of a physical qubit is changed on purpose.

## Experience-level scope

**Beginner (V1) teaches:** what Module 02 assumed; levels of a real object and the choice of two; the ladder as one picture; natural and engineered qubits in one sentence each; the five needs; starting in a known state and why heat against the gap decides how reliably; a drive that must single out the qubit's levels; isolated yet reachable; a physical qubit against the ideal and the logical; no real numbers, and no device pictures.

**Intermediate (future):** energy-level diagrams with frequencies; the thermal weighting as a formula; anharmonicity and the transmon as the engineered answer; the two-level approximation and its limits; the standard criteria (DiVincenzo's) named.

**Advanced (future):** Hamiltonians and qubit design.

Do not build Intermediate or Advanced content to make V1 more complete.

**Not on this page (any level):** how a gate is applied or how large a rotation is (04.01.02); how a qubit is read (04.01.03); T1, T2, or lifetimes (04.01.04); sources and kinds of noise (04.01.05); any one platform's construction or any device picture (04.02); logical qubits, encoding, and error correction (Module 07); how software describes a device (Module 05); real temperatures, frequencies, device names, or figures.

## Page structure (draft)

1. **What the Ideal Qubit Got for Free.** Reconnect to Module 02. List, in a short table, what the ideal model assumed: it starts in |0⟩, a gate does exactly what it says, a measurement tells the truth, it lasts as long as you like, nothing else touches it. Ask: "What does a real object have to do to be a qubit?" Define **physical qubit** in one sentence (a real object, or part of one, that is used as a qubit). Say that this page is about the first things it has to do.

2. **Many Levels, Two Chosen.** Introduce the **energy ladder** with a small static diagram: a real system can be in one of several distinct levels, each with its own energy. A qubit uses **two** of them as |0⟩ and |1⟩. The other levels are still there. Say the ladder is the picture for atoms, ions, and engineered circuits, and that not every qubit is made of levels of different energy (one sentence, with photon polarization as the example, to be shown in 04.02.04). State the general requirement in plain words. Say the system on this page is invented.

3. **Natural and Engineered Qubits.** Two short cards (Open question 7). Natural: an atom or ion, whose levels nature fixes, so they are all alike. Engineered: a circuit built to behave like an atom, whose levels depend on how it was made, so each is slightly different. One consequence each. No platform detail.

4. **Five Needs.** The checklist, in plain words, each with a short sentence: **start** in a known state; **control** it, meaning change its state on purpose; **connect** it to other qubits so they can affect each other when we want; **read** it, meaning find out what state it is in; **keep** it, meaning its state lasts long enough to use. Say which page takes up each. Say that this page tests the first two.

5. **Main Interactive: "Choose a Qubit."** See below.

6. **Starting in a Known State.** Explain what the first result of the explorer shows. A system left alone settles toward its lowest levels, and heat jostles it back up, so the qubit starts reliably when the gap is big compared with the heat. Cooling is one way; others exist and are named on the platform pages. The recipe is the same each time (02.01) and the result is a share, not a certainty. Keep it to words and the bars; no formula.

7. **Being Controllable: Singling Out Two Levels.** Explain the second result. A drive is tuned to the step between the two lowest levels. If other steps are the same size, the drive moves the system there too, and it escapes the qubit's two levels (leakage, returned to in 04.01.05). Unevenly spaced levels let the drive single out the pair. This is why a good qubit is built or chosen so that its levels are not evenly spaced. State the tolerance as invented. Say the page has not yet said how the drive works or how large a rotation is; that is 04.01.02.

8. **Isolated, Yet Reachable.** The callout and two-sided diagram (Open question 8). One sentence on the Module 07 fence: a physical qubit is one such object; qubits made from many are taught in Module 07 (Open question 9).

9. **From a Qubit That Starts to a Qubit That Moves.** Bridge. Recap in the form of the table from subsection 1, now with a third column: what the real object has to provide, and the page that takes it up. Ask: "You have a qubit that starts in |0⟩ and a drive that can single out its two levels. How exactly do you move its state, and how far?" End with the question and `Next: Gates and Control →`. Do not explain pulses or rotation angles here.

## Main Interactive: "Choose a Qubit"

- **Controls (what the learner changes):**
  - spacing: evenly spaced, slightly uneven (2% smaller each step), clearly uneven (10% smaller each step), all invented;
  - gap: the first step, small, medium, large (invented units);
  - temperature: warm, cool, cold (invented units);
  - Reset (back to the starting settings: evenly spaced, medium gap, warm, which fail both tests so the learner has something to fix).
- **Display, kept in three separate places:**
  - **the system** (shown): the five-level ladder, with the two qubit levels marked |0⟩ and |1⟩ and the other levels marked "outside the qubit", the steps between levels labelled with their sizes, and the heat shown as a labelled marker;
  - **the results** (derived): the starting shares (|0⟩, |1⟩, outside the qubit) as bars with percentages and a sentence, and the per-step response to a drive tuned to the first step (responds / does not respond, with the difference from the first step);
  - **the verdict** (observed): the five needs, **Start** and **Control** with their result, **Connect**, **Read**, and **Keep** as "not tested on this page" with their page, and the closing sentence.
- **Cause, response, observation:** spacing, gap, and temperature are the cause; the shares and per-step responses are the response; the verdict is what the learner takes away.
- **Rules the page keeps:** the system is labelled **invented**; units are invented; no kelvin, hertz, or device; Start and Control are labelled **computed from a simplified model**; Connect, Read, and Keep are never shown as passed; pass and fail use a glyph and a word, not green or red; the qubit is the two lowest levels in this model, and the page says other choices exist.
- **Accessibility:** radio groups for the three controls; the ladder has a text description and the results a real table; an aria-live region announces the new shares and which steps respond when a setting changes; focus stays where it was after a re-render.
- **Scope fence:** no drive timing or rotation angle, no readout mechanism, no lifetimes, no noise kinds, no platform, no real numbers.

### Checks

- *Prediction task:* "You keep the gap the same and make the system colder. Does the share that starts in |0⟩ go up, go down, or stay the same?" (up); then compare with the explorer.
- *Conceptual question:* "Is a physical qubit the whole atom or circuit?" with feedback on each wrong choice (no: it is the two levels chosen to stand for 0 and 1; the other levels are still there).
- *Transfer task:* "A designer builds a system whose steps are all the same size. It starts reliably when cold. Why is it still a poor qubit?" (a drive tuned to the first step would move the system between other levels too, so it can't be controlled as a qubit), and then "which need did it fail?" (control).

## Important pedagogical constraints (draft)

Do not imply:
- `A PHYSICAL QUBIT = AN ATOM, ION, OR CIRCUIT`. It is two chosen states of one, and the other levels are still there.
- `EVERY QUBIT IS THE LOWEST TWO LEVELS OF A LADDER`, or `EVERY QUBIT IS MADE OF LEVELS OF DIFFERENT ENERGY`. The ladder is one picture; a photon's polarization is a counterexample.
- `A DEVICE MUST BE COLD`. Cooling is one way to start in a known state; others exist.
- `THE START IS PERFECT`. It is a share, never a certainty.
- `START + CONTROL = A GOOD QUBIT`. Connect, read, and keep are not tested here.
- `ENGINEERED = LESS REAL`, or `NATURAL = BETTER`. They differ in how alike they are.
- `THE PAGE'S NUMBERS = REAL DEVICE VALUES`. They are invented and labelled.
- `A PHYSICAL QUBIT = A LOGICAL QUBIT`.
- That the qubit is a ball on a ladder, or that a level is a place in space.
- That passing the explorer's checks is how real qubits are chosen. It is a simplified model of two of the reasons.

Do not teach here: gates or rotation angles, readout, lifetimes, noise kinds, any one platform, error correction, how software describes a device, or real numbers.
