**Weight of this page:** it closes Hardware Fundamentals, it owns the module's central distinction (**noise source ≠ error ≠ reported metric**), and it is where the pages' loose ends meet: a miscalibration that "drifts" (04.01.02), a threshold that "has to be found again" (04.01.03), a T that "changes" (04.01.04), and leakage "returned to later" (04.01.01). Six risks to watch:
- **A catalogue of physics.** Each kind of noise has a literature of its own. The draft keeps to **five kinds, one line each**, with no materials, no spectra, and no platform causes (those are 04.02 and Intermediate).
- **Invented shares that look like facts.** An error budget with percentages invites "so control is the biggest problem". Every share is invented and labelled, no ranking of sources is implied, and the page shows that the largest source **changes** with the qubit, the operation, and the time (Open questions 2 and 3).
- **Switching a source off.** A model can remove a source and a device cannot. The page says so (Open question 4).
- **A drift model read as a law.** A steady drift per step is a teaching device. Module 07 says not to read a universal rate from an example, and this page says the same (Open question 5).
- **The reported figure hiding the budget.** A device reports one number for an operation, which lumps every source together and cannot say which dominated (Open question 4).
- **Three neighbours.** Module 05 owns how software is told about calibration, Module 07 owns the error vocabulary and models, and 04.02 owns each platform's causes. This page names each fence once (Open question 8).

**Why a new shared model is needed:** the page needs an error share for each of five sources for each of three operations on three invented qubits, a combination of them, a drift of one setting with time, a tune that re-runs an earlier experiment, and a measured reported figure. Most of the ingredients exist (`QedCoherence` for the environment, `QedDrive` for the control, `QedReadout` for the readout), and none combines them. 04.02.06 reuses the budget (a platform's usual dominant source).

## Open questions for review

Each has a recommendation. Say which you accept or change; the rest of the draft already assumes the recommendations.

1. **What is the one idea the page teaches?** The taxonomy row says "where imperfection comes from, why it varies and drifts, and why devices are re-tuned". *Recommendation:* **an operation goes wrong for several different reasons at once. Each reason is a source (the environment, the control, the neighbours, leakage, and the readout). Each source causes an error, which is a share of the runs of that operation that go wrong. And the number a device reports is a measurement of the errors added together. Sources differ from qubit to qubit and from operation to operation, and the settings that tame them drift, so a device measures its figures and tunes its settings again and again.** Four supporting points, shown and not derived: five kinds of source; source, error, and reported figure are three different things; qubits differ and settings drift; and the loop of measure, set, use, drift, and measure again.

2. **What does the main interaction show, and how does it relate to the taxonomy's wording?** The taxonomy says "switch sources on and off and see which dominates an operation; move time forward and watch a parameter drift until the device is recalibrated". *Recommendation:* an **Error Budget** explorer on the three invented qubits of 04.01.04 and the Bell circuit's three operations (a one-qubit signal, a two-qubit gate, and a readout, with the invented lengths 1, 4, and 10 from the 04.01.02 timeline), with these controls:
   - **Qubit:** A, B, or C (the same invented T1 and T2 as 04.01.04, plus the invented numbers of Question 3).
   - **Operation:** the one-qubit signal, the two-qubit gate, or the readout.
   - **Sources:** five checkboxes (Environment, Control, Neighbours, Leakage, Readout), all on at the start. A source that does not apply to the chosen operation is shown as "not applicable" and never hidden (the module's rule for a gap).
   - **Time:** a slider for the time now, 0 to 48 in invented time steps, with a typed value, clamped. Default 0.
   - **Measure the reported figure** (1,000 simulated runs of the operation), **Recalibrate** (Question 6), and **Reset** (qubit A, the one-qubit signal, all sources on, time 0, freshly tuned).

   Shown in three separate places: the **settings** (the controls and the time of the last tune); the **model's exact answer** (one labelled row for each source with its error share, the total, the source that is largest, and a drift plot of the control error against the time since the last tune, each tagged "computed from a simplified model"); and the **observation**, only after the learner asks (the reported figure from 1,000 runs, and the result of a tune, each tagged "simulated"). One bar for each source, with its label and its percentage, is drawn **instead of one stacked bar**, so that no source depends on a colour to be told apart. The taxonomy's wording is a design intention, and this refines it; please confirm.

3. **How are the five sources defined, and where does each number come from?** *Recommendation:* each source is the **error share of the chosen operation**, the share of its runs that go wrong because of that source, and each comes from a model that already exists or from a labelled invented number:
   - **Environment:** from `QedCoherence`. The energy loss and phase loss over the operation's length, from the qubit's T1 and T2 (the average of the two for a gate; for a readout only the energy loss matters, and only for a 1, so half the runs). Applies to all three operations.
   - **Control:** from `QedDrive`. The half turn's offset from the strength now in force against the half-turn length stored at the last tune, as the chance that the qubit does not end in |1⟩ (as 04.01.02's "never exactly right" table computes). A two-qubit gate is twice as sensitive to the same offset (an invented factor, labelled). **Not applicable** to a readout, whose own setting belongs to the Readout source.
   - **Neighbours:** an invented share for each qubit and each operation, standing for **crosstalk**, "an operation on one qubit disturbing the others". Applies to all three, and is largest for the two-qubit gate.
   - **Leakage:** an invented share for each qubit and each operation, standing for a qubit leaving its two levels (04.01.01's evenly spaced ladder was the way in). Applies to the two gates, **not applicable** to a readout.
   - **Readout:** from `QedReadout`. The average of the two misreads at the middle threshold for Medium signals (about 2.3%). Applies to the readout only.

   The shares **add** when they are small, and the exact combination (one minus the product of the chances of going right) is what the module computes. The page says "added up". The page does not show a formula. A design rule for the final spec: **every source is the largest somewhere** in the grid of qubit, operation, and time, and none is the largest everywhere, so the learner has something to find and no pattern to memorise. The invented numbers are fixed in the final spec so that this holds, and a test pins it.

4. **How are "noise source ≠ error ≠ reported figure" and the thought experiment of a switched-off source kept honest?** This is the module's central distinction (taxonomy sections 8 and 13). *Recommendation:* a **three-line table** early on, each line with an example from the explorer: the **source** is what disturbs (the environment), the **error** is the share of runs that go wrong because of it (a number for this operation), and the **reported figure** is what a device measures by repeating the operation and counting how often it went right (its gate fidelity; for a readout, its readout fidelity). In the explorer:

It then says the model's own value, and that "the figure moves from run to run". It says that **the reported figure is one number: it cannot say which source dominated**.
   - The page says plainly that **switching a source off is something only this model can do**. A real device cannot remove a source, so it finds out which dominates by running more experiments (randomized benchmarking is **named** here as a method, and it is Intermediate).
   - "Metric" is the taxonomy's word and "reported figure" is the word the earlier pages used. The page uses "reported figure" and says once that this is the "reported metric" of Module 05.

5. **What drifts, how, and what is said about why?** *Recommendation:* **one setting drifts in the explorer: the strength the qubit gets for a given signal** (04.01.02's miscalibration, now changing with time). Each qubit drifts by a fixed invented share of strength per time step, in its own direction and at its own pace, so the offset between the strength now and the strength at the last tune grows steadily until a tune. The page says, once, that this is **a teaching model**: real drift is not steady, depends on the platform and the setting, and has no universal rate (Module 07 says the same). It says what the learner can know about why: the settings of the control equipment and the qubit's own properties shift slowly with their surroundings, which nobody holds perfectly still. It does **not** name causes. The same loop applies to the other settings the earlier pages found: the readout threshold (04.01.03) and the times (04.01.04), each by its own experiment, shown in a **three-row table** (the setting, the experiment, the page that taught it) and not as three more drifting parameters.

6. **What does Recalibrate do, and what does it leave out?** The taxonomy says the page "closes the loop to calibration by re-running the earlier experiments". *Recommendation:* **Recalibrate** re-runs **04.01.02's drive-and-flip experiment** (the seeded sweep over 25 lengths, 100 shots each) at the strength now in force, takes the middle of the lengths with the most 1s, **stores it as the new half-turn length**, and sets the time of the last tune to now. ## Goal

Teach the learner that an operation goes wrong for several different reasons at once, that each source is not the same as the error it causes or the figure a device reports, that sources differ from qubit to qubit and settings drift with time, and that this is why a device is measured and tuned again and again.

Central progression:

`A QUBIT YOU CAN START, TURN, READ, AND KEEP → AN OPERATION THAT STILL GOES WRONG → FIVE KINDS OF SOURCE → SOURCE ≠ ERROR ≠ REPORTED FIGURE → QUBITS THAT DIFFER → A SETTING THAT DRIFTS → MEASURE, SET, USE, DRIFT, MEASURE AGAIN → TO BE CONTINUED`

Key conceptual transition: **"Module 02's operations were exact and its numbers were certain. On a device, several sources disturb every operation, each qubit differs, the settings drift, and the figure a device reports is one measured number for all of it."**

It opens from the qubit the learner can now start, turn, read, and keep, and ends by asking how different physical systems answer the same questions.

## Learning outcomes (Beginner)

The learner should be able to:

1. Say that an operation goes wrong for several reasons at once.
2. Name five kinds of source (the environment, the control, the neighbours, leakage, and the readout), and say in one line what each is.
3. Distinguish a noise source from an error and from a reported figure, with an example of each.
4. Read an error budget and say which source is largest for an operation.
5. Say that the largest source changes with the qubit, the operation, and the time.
6. Say that the reported figure is one number for all the sources together, and cannot say which one dominated.
7. Say that a real device cannot switch a source off, and how it finds out which dominates.
8. Say that qubits on one device differ from one another, and that their numbers change.
9. Say that a setting drifts over time, and that the drift in this page is a teaching model with no universal rate.
10. Describe the loop of measure, set, use, drift, and measure again, and name the three experiments it re-runs.
11. Say that a tune is itself a measurement and leaves a small residual.
12. Say that a reported figure is a measurement at one moment and not a guarantee, and ask how different physical systems answer these questions.

## Experience-level scope

**Beginner (V1) teaches:** five kinds of source, one line each; source, error, and reported figure; an error budget for three operations on three invented qubits; qubits that differ; a steady drift of one setting; a tune that re-runs the drive-and-flip experiment; the loop and the three experiments it re-runs; a reported figure that hides the budget; the five needs recapped with how each goes wrong; no real numbers.

**Intermediate (future):** defects and flux noise; leakage in detail; crosstalk matrices; randomized benchmarking named and interpreted.

**Advanced (future):** how noise models are extracted.

Do not build Intermediate or Advanced content to make V1 more complete.

**Not on this page (any level):** how a gate turns a qubit (04.01.02); how a qubit is read (04.01.03); how a state fades and what T1 and T2 time (04.01.04); any platform's sources, numbers, or construction (04.02); the error vocabulary, noise models, codes, and decoding (Module 07); how software describes, receives, or responds to calibration data (Module 05); a schedule for recalibration; real numbers, drift rates, or fidelities; cross-module links beyond the few essential ones (deferred by the taxonomy).

## Pedagogical constraints (draft)

- Do not imply that a noise source is the same as an error, or an error the same as the figure a device reports.
- Do not imply that one source always dominates, or that the invented shares are real. The largest source changes with the qubit, the operation, and the time.
- Do not imply that a device can switch a source off. Only the model can.
- Do not imply that the reported figure can say which source dominated.
- Do not imply that drift is steady or has a universal rate. It is a teaching model.
- Do not imply that a recalibration makes an error zero. The tune is a measurement and leaves a residual.
- Do not imply that the three qubits are real, or that qubits on one device are alike.
- Do not imply that a reported figure is a guarantee. It is a measurement at one moment.
- Do not imply that crosstalk and coupling are the same. One is intended, the other is not.
- Do not teach any platform's sources, the error vocabulary or noise models, how software responds to calibration, a recalibration schedule, or real numbers here.
