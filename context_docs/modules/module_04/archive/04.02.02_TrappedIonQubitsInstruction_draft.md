- **"Any pair" is not "free".** Any two ions in a chain can interact through shared motion, but a longer chain makes that harder, and the page must show the cost, not only the benefit (Open question 7).
- **Vendors.** Two of the sources describe commercial machines. They are cited by journal and authors only, and no machine, company, or record appears on the page.
- **Neighbours.** Module 05 owns the connectivity map and native gates as software sees them, and Module 07 the error vocabulary. This page names each fence once.

## Open questions for review

Each has a recommendation. Say which you accept or change; the rest of the draft already assumes the recommendations.

1. **What is the one idea the page teaches?** The taxonomy row says "how single ions held by electric fields act as qubits and what that implies". *Recommendation:* **a trapped-ion qubit is one real atom, given an electric charge so that electric fields can hold it still in a vacuum, with two of its own long-lived internal levels standing for 0 and 1. Light cools its motion, controls it, and reads it out. Because the ions sit together in a chain and share their motion, any two can interact. The price is slow operations, and a chain that gets harder to use as it grows.** Four supporting points, shown and not derived: it is **natural** (every ion of one kind is identical, the opposite of 04.02.01's "no two alike"), it is **held by fields and cooled by light** (not by a refrigerator), it is **slow with a very long memory** (a trade-off, not a verdict), and **longer chains are harder, so devices move ions between regions**. Cross-platform comparison is 04.02.06's job, and this page does not rank.

2. **What is the "compare with" control, and how is it kept from becoming a ranking?** Taxonomy decision 11 asks each platform page to "offer a compare with control", and 04.02.01's final spec promised it would "appear when a second card exists". *Recommendation:* on the page's **Platform Card section** (and on 04.02.01's, added in the same build) a **"Compare with" selector** lists only platforms whose cards exist, starting at "None". Choosing one shows the **two cards side by side**, in the same six rows and the same order, this page's platform first. Rules, all pinned by tests: **no sorting, no highlight of a larger or smaller figure, no chart, no bar, no verdict or "better/worse" word, the same visual weight for both**, every figure tagged "approximate", the **computed operations that fit** shown for both with its label ("computed from typical values in the sources; the ends of the ranges are not one device"), and the standing note "A side-by-side reading is not a ranking. Figures change quickly, and the ends of a range are not one device." Where two platforms word a row differently (the ion card measures a phase-coherence time, T2, where 04.02.01's measures energy loss and phase loss) the row shows **each platform's own label** and a one-line note that the two are not the same quantity. On a phone each question stacks the two answers. **The picture of speed against lifetime, the priority selector, and any "what each costs" synthesis are 04.02.06's**, and are not built here. *Why not wait for 04.02.06:* the taxonomy gives every platform page the control, and a learner who has just read two cards will compare them whether or not the page lets them.

3. **How are sources numbered when a second page cites a different set?** `QedPlatform.REFERENCES` is one numbered list, in the order 04.02.01 first cites its sources, and each figure stores **numbers**. A second page cannot share that numbering, and the compare view mixes two pages' sources. *Recommendation:* **store each reference once under an id** (for example `krantz2019`), give each platform a **`sources` list of ids in the order its page first cites them**, and **compute the number from that list**, so each page numbers its own Sources list from 1 in first-citation order (the MRS rule). The **compare view builds its own numbering** over the sources of the cards it shows, in order of first citation, with its own Sources list directly beneath it, so no number is ever ambiguous. **04.02.01's page output does not change** (the same seven entries, the same numbers), and its tests are kept and extended. *Why not copy the list per page:* the same paper would then be stored twice, and the comparison page, which cites both sets, would have no single source of truth.

4. **Which figures does the page state, and how are they kept honest?** *Recommendation:* **only the figures the Platform Card needs, as approximate ranges, each cited, and each flagged for hardware review.** The ranges below were **proposed from the sources, not from general knowledge**, and were **checked on 2026-11-07 against the primary papers listed after them**. They are written with "roughly", as ranges, and never as one number:
   - **Where the ion is held:** an ion is held in **ultra-high vacuum** by electric fields; the trap is **often at room temperature** (some are cooled), and the ion's **motion is laser-cooled** to the equivalent of **roughly a millikelvin, and lower with further cooling (tens of microkelvin)**. *As a range:* roughly **tens of microkelvin to a millikelvin** for the motion.
   - **One-qubit gate time:** **roughly microseconds to tens of microseconds**. *Sources: a high-fidelity gate studied from about 1 to 60 µs with its best near 7.5 µs; a transport-based device with about 70 µs.*
   - **Two-qubit gate time:** **roughly microseconds to hundreds of microseconds**, with a typical core of tens of microseconds. *Sources: "typically 1 to 100 µs", the fastest high-fidelity gate 1.6 µs, a gate studied from 3.8 to 520 µs (a 99.9% gate at 50 µs), and about 80 µs in a transport-based device.*
   - **Readout time:** **roughly tens to hundreds of microseconds**. *Sources: readout above 99.99% in under 200 µs, and 99.93% in 11 µs; about 360 µs for the measurement step of a transport-based device.*
   - **Coherence time, T2:** **roughly seconds to minutes**, with the best laboratory memory **over ten minutes**. *Sources: 0.2 s for optical qubits up to 600 s for hyperfine qubits, 50 s without dynamical decoupling, 6 s measured in a gate experiment, and over 10 minutes at room temperature. **These are T2 times, limited by technical dephasing (stray magnetic fields) and not by the fundamental lifetime of the state**, so the card labels the row "Coherence time (T2)" and not "T1 and T2".* The typical core used for the computation is **one to ten seconds**. **This is the least well supported figure:** the 6 s measurement and the "up to" values are stated, but no source read gives one typical value, so the expert should confirm the lower end.
   - **Operations that fit in a lifetime:** computed from the two typical cores (one to ten seconds over ten to a hundred microseconds), roughly **ten thousand to a million**, shown as an order of magnitude (**"tens of thousands to a million or more"**) and labelled "computed from typical values in the sources; the ends of the ranges are not one device". The page does **not** repeat any source's own cross-platform comparison.
   - **No count is stated for how many ions fit in one chain.** One source says a single crystal is unlikely to scale past about a hundred qubits. That is the authors' claim, it is contestable, and the page says only that a longer chain is harder, which is the same rule 04.02.01 kept ("does not predict a limit").

   **Sources checked** (journal, volume, and first page verified against publisher or index records on 2026-11-07; the papers were read in preprint form, and the published versions were not compared line by line). They would appear at the bottom of the page in the MRS style, numbered in order of first citation, with no vendor or machine named:
   - C.D. Bruzewicz, J. Chiaverini, R. McConnell, and J.M. Sage, *Appl. Phys. Rev.* **6**, 021314 (2019). Trap at room temperature, laser-cooled motion near 1 mK and 20 µK, coherence from 0.2 s to 600 s, two-qubit gates of 1 to 100 µs (fastest 1.6 µs), readout above 99.99% in under 200 µs and 99.93% in 11 µs, identical ions, slow gates, chains slowed by many motional modes, ions held for hours to months.
   - C.J. Ballance, T.P. Harty, N.M. Linke, M.A. Sepiol, and D.M. Lucas, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **117**, 060504 (2016). Room-temperature trap, ultra-high vacuum, single-qubit gates from 0.9 to 60 µs (best near 7.5 µs), two-qubit gates from 3.8 to 520 µs, T2 of 6 s.
   - J.M. Pino et al., *Nature* **592**, 209 (2021). A transport-based device: single-qubit gate about 70 µs, two-qubit gate about 80 µs, measurement about 360 µs, moving ions about a millisecond, cooling after moves, and a cryogenic trap.
   - Y. Wang et al., *Nat. Photonics* **11**, 646 (2017). A single ion's coherence over 10 minutes at room temperature, with magnetic-field noise as the limit.

   The page carries a **"figures change quickly" note** and, as on 04.02.01, a **visible numbered Sources list at the bottom of the page in the MRS style**. **No vendor, product, or machine name, no record qubit count, and no ranking** appear. FLAGS records that the facts are checked against these papers but not yet reviewed by a hardware expert, and that the lower end of the typical lifetime is the weakest row.

5. **How are "what is the qubit" and "held in empty space, cooled by light" taught, and what happens to "cold"?** *Recommendation:* **the qubit is one real atom with an electric charge** (an **ion**), so that electric fields can hold it. It is held still **in a vacuum**, in a device called an **ion trap** (the kind of trap is named once and not derived). Two of the atom's **own internal levels**, long-lived ones, stand for 0 and 1 (the type of level is named once: "hyperfine" or "optical" at most, and no ion species is named). **Every ion of one kind is identical**, because nature, and not fabrication, sets its levels. This is the contrast with 04.02.01, and the page says **identical does not mean error-free** (the fields and the light that control it still vary). **On "cold":** the page says the ion's **motion** is slowed with laser light, called **laser cooling**, until it is nearly still, equivalent to roughly a millikelvin or less, and that the trap itself is often at room temperature, with some cooled. So the module's "cold is not universal" gets its careful form: **a superconducting circuit is cooled as a whole by a refrigerator, and an ion's motion is cooled by light**. Reuse `QedLadder` only as a one-line link back to 04.01.01 (no new ladder picture), since the levels here are given by nature and the page should not suggest an engineered ladder. No equations, no species, no trap theory.

6. **How are control and readout taught, and how are the earlier pages kept consistent?** *Recommendation:* **control:** an ion is turned by **light** (a laser, focused on one ion) or by **microwaves**, tuned to the gap between its two levels, for a chosen strength and time: the "microwave signals" and "drive" rows of 04.01.02, now with this platform's version. Focused light can pick out one ion in a chain; microwaves reach all of them. Say that the shaping of the pulses and the details of the light are for a later level. **Readout:** shine light that **only one of the two states scatters**. If the ion scatters it, it shines (**bright**); if not, it stays **dark**. A detector collects the scattered photons and counts them. The count is the 04.01.03 **overlap and threshold again**: a bright ion gives a high count, a dark ion a low one, and the counts can overlap, so a misread is possible. Use **an invented photon-count model** (Poisson counts with invented means, seeded, labelled invented and simulated) and the page's **bright/dark glyph with the word**, never colour alone. The convention "dark is 0 and bright is 1" is stated as the page's own. No detector physics, no camera or photon-collection details.

7. **How is "how do qubits connect" taught, and where does Module 05 begin?** *Recommendation:* **ions in a chain share their motion.** The ions sit in a line, held by the same fields, and they **move together like a row of weights on a spring**, so light that touches two of them can link their qubits through that shared motion (the analogy is named once and is not derived: no modes). The result is that **any pair in the chain can interact directly**, which is the opposite of 04.02.01's fixed neighbours. The page shows the **cost, not only the benefit**: a **longer chain has more ways to move, so an interaction gets slower and harder to do well** (shown in the explorer with an **invented** rule, labelled). Module 05's fence is named once: that a device can describe "every pair" in its connectivity, and what software does with that, are Module 05. Do not duplicate `QedBackend`'s field catalog.

8. **How are speed and lifetime taught, and how is the Today page's promise kept?** *Recommendation:* the card's fourth question shows the ranges of Question 4 and the computed operations-that-fit, in the same wording as 04.01.04 and 04.02.01 ("a budget, not a promise"; "a long time is not a guarantee"). The Today page's promise is kept for this platform: its qubits **remember much longer and its operations are slower**, said as a **trade-off in this platform's own terms** ("slow operations leave a very long memory to fit many of them in"), with the **side-by-side reading offered through the compare control** and the **synthesis left to 04.02.06**. The page labels the row "Coherence time (T2)" and says in one line that the long times are **phase-coherence times limited by stray fields**, not the state's fundamental lifetime. No ranking, no "better".

9. **How is "what makes it hard to scale" taught, honestly?** *Recommendation:* **three reasons, each one line plus a small picture or an invented number, with no stated chain length.** (a) **Longer chains are harder:** more ions means more ways for the chain to move, so interactions slow down and get harder to do well (the explorer's invented rule). (b) **Light has to reach every ion:** each ion needs light of the right kind aimed at it, and more ions need more of that. (c) **Moving ions between regions is one way to scale:** keep several short chains and **move ions** between zones to bring a pair together. The page states the **cost**: moving takes time and disturbs the ion's motion, so it must be cooled again, which an invented toggle can show (**if practical**, otherwise one sentence and a static picture). Say that how any of this is being worked on is not a Beginner topic, and that the page does not predict a limit.

10. **How are the Bell strip and "what goes wrong here" shown, without ranking or inventing?** *Recommendation:* the module's strip, **"how would this platform do it?"**, for the Bell circuit's three operations: a **one-qubit rotation** (a light or microwave pulse on the ion), a **two-qubit interaction** (light on two ions, through their shared motion), and a **readout** (light scattered, bright or dark). Reuse `QedCircuit`. Then **one short section on what usually goes wrong here**, tied to 04.01.05's five sources, in words and without numbers: the **environment** (stray magnetic fields, and the ion's motion being heated, which limit the memory), the **control** (the strength, aim, and timing of the light), the **neighbours** (light meant for one ion reaching the next, and ions disturbing each other), **leakage** (the ion ends in a level outside its two), and the **readout** (overlapping photon counts). Say plainly that **which source is largest depends on the device and changes over time**, and that this page ranks none. This section is **flagged for hardware review**.

11. **What does the main interaction show, and how does it relate to the taxonomy's wording?** The taxonomy says "Platform Card plus an ion-chain explorer: choose two ions to interact and see read-out as bright or dark". *Recommendation:* an **Ion Chain** explorer on a **schematic** (not to scale, labelled simplified): a row of ions held between trap electrodes, inside a dashed vacuum boundary, with a detector below. The learner:

   - **resets**.
   In separate places the page shows **the picture** (shown), **the interaction** (derived: the pair chosen, the invented time of the interaction for this chain length, labelled invented, and the words "any pair can interact"), and **the readout** (observed, only after the learner asks: for each ion, bright or dark and its count, tagged simulated, with the two ions of the pair agreeing and the others dark). ## Goal

Teach the learner that a trapped-ion qubit is one real atom held still by electric fields in a vacuum, with two of its own long-lived levels standing for 0 and 1, cooled, controlled, and read out with light, slow with a very long memory, linked to any other ion in its chain through shared motion, and harder to use as the chain grows, and that this is a second answer to the same six questions every platform has to answer.

Central progression:

`THE SIX QUESTIONS → A REAL ATOM HELD BY FIELDS → COOLED, CONTROLLED, AND READ WITH LIGHT → SLOW, WITH A LONG MEMORY → ANY PAIR IN A CHAIN → LONGER CHAINS ARE HARDER → THE BELL CIRCUIT'S THREE OPERATIONS HERE → TWO CARDS SIDE BY SIDE → TO BE CONTINUED`

Key conceptual transition: **"The same six questions have a very different answer here: the qubit is a real atom, held by fields and cooled by light, and its cost is not wiring but speed and the length of the chain."**

It opens from the superconducting card and ends by handing the questions to a platform that holds many atoms with light.

## Learning outcomes (Beginner)

The learner should be able to:

1. Say that a trapped-ion qubit is one real atom with an electric charge, held still by electric fields in a vacuum, with two of its own levels standing for 0 and 1.
2. Say that every ion of one kind is identical by nature, and that this does not make it error-free.
3. Say how the ion's motion is cooled, by light, and that the trap itself is often at room temperature, so that "cold is not universal" has a careful form.
4. Say that the ion is controlled by light or microwaves tuned to its two levels.
5. Say that it is read by whether it scatters light, bright or dark, and that the photon counts overlap so a misread is possible.
6. Say, in roughly stated terms, that its operations are slow and its memory very long, and read an operations-that-fit range as a budget and not a promise.
7. Say that any two ions in a chain can interact through shared motion, and that a longer chain makes this harder.
8. Give three reasons it is hard to scale: longer chains, light to every ion, and the cost of moving ions between regions.
9. Say how the Bell circuit's three operations are done on this platform.
10. Say which of 04.01.05's five sources of noise the device brings, and that which one is largest depends on the device and the time.
11. Read the Platform Card's six questions for this platform, use the compare control to read two cards side by side without ranking them, and say that the figures are approximate ranges that change quickly.
12. Find the sources behind the figures at the bottom of the page.
13. Ask how a different physical system, with many uncharged atoms held by light, would answer the same questions.

## Main Interactive: "Ion Chain"

- **Controls (what the learner changes):**
  - chain length: one of four invented counts (for example 3, 6, 9, and 12), chosen by a real radio group;
  - the first ion and the second ion: two real selectors, and by selecting ions in the picture (any two different ions are allowed, the nearest or the farthest);
  - **Rotate the first ion**, **Interact the pair**, and **Read out**: three real buttons, in the order of the Bell circuit's three operations (a later button is available only after the earlier ones, with a short note saying why);
  - **Reset**.
- **Display, kept in three separate places:**
  - **the schematic** (shown): the chain of ions between trap electrodes, inside a dashed vacuum boundary, with the light on the chosen ion (control blue, double outline), the shared-motion link between the chosen pair (gray, labelled "shared motion"), and the detector below (read-out brown, dash-dot, with an arrow from the ions to it), all labelled "simplified, not to scale";
  - **the interaction** (derived): the chosen pair, the **invented** time of the interaction for this chain length (labelled invented, and growing with the length), and the words "any pair can interact";
  - **the readout** (observed, only after Read out): for every ion, **bright** or **dark** (a glyph and the word) and its photon count, tagged simulated, with the chosen pair agreeing and the other ions dark, and a line that the counts overlap, so a misread is rare but possible.
- **Rules the page keeps:** the picture is a schematic and not a trap; no vendor, no real device, no ion species, no qubit count of a real machine; every number is invented or simulated and tagged; the control and read-out cues are the module's own; no ion depends on a colour to be bright or dark; nothing is animated.
- **Accessibility:** real buttons, selectors, and radios; a text table of the ions with the same facts as the picture; a text description of the picture; the result announced in an aria-live region after each step; focus stays where it was; tables are real tables with stacked rows on a phone.
- **Scope fence:** no derivation, no trap theory, no motional modes, no species, no other platform, no ranking, no real figures beyond the card's approximate ranges.

### Checks

- *Prediction task:* "You choose the first and the last ion in a long chain to interact. Can they interact, and what does a longer chain do to the interaction?" (yes, any pair can; and a longer chain makes it slower and harder), then compare with the explorer.
- *Conceptual question:* "An ion is read as dark. Does that mean the ion is not there?" with feedback on each wrong choice (no: dark is one of the two results, 0, and an ion in 0 scatters little light; the counts can overlap, so a misread is possible).

## Pedagogical constraints (draft)

- Do not imply that a trapped-ion qubit is the "better" or "worse" platform than a superconducting one, or that either is the standard. It is a second answer to the same six questions, and its answers have costs.
- Do not imply that being identical makes an ion error-free. Gates, fields, light, and the readout still disturb it.
- Do not imply that "any pair can interact" means interactions are free. A longer chain is harder.
- Do not imply that "slow with a long memory" is a verdict. It is a trade-off, and the comparison is 04.02.06's.
- Do not imply that a range is a device, or that the ends of a range are one device. Every figure changes quickly.
- Do not imply that the coherence times are lifetimes of the state. They are phase-coherence times, limited by technical dephasing.
- Do not imply that the schematic is a real trap, or that the chain model's numbers are real.
- Do not imply that the sources of noise on this platform are ranked. Which is largest depends on the device and the time.
- Do not imply that the compare control ranks, sorts, or picks a winner. It is a side-by-side reading of the same six questions.
- Do not name a vendor, a product, a machine, an ion species, a record qubit count, or a single device figure, and do not state how many ions a chain can hold.
- Do not teach trap theory, motional modes, gate schemes, pulse shaping, routing, coupling maps, error vocabulary, or any other platform here.
